# What sets Aafno apart

Most language apps optimise for time-in-app: short drills, recognition tasks, and a streak. Aafno optimises for use. The following differences shape the product. They are product requirements, not slogans.

## Structured grammar

Grammar is taught. A learner should be able to say what changed in a sentence and why: the honorific, the tense, the postposition, the agreement. Rules are attached to sentences they can say, then reused. A pattern is not “done” because it appeared once in a tip.

## Sentence construction

Aafno treats building a sentence as its own skill. Learners assemble and vary sentences, swap slots, and repair a sentence that is almost right. Memorising a line is allowed as a start. It is not the goal. The goal is a sentence the learner has not been shown, made from parts they understand.

## Real conversational practice

Practice is dialogue: questions that expect an answer, answers that expect a follow-up, and the small repairs people actually make (“हजुर?”, a softer honorific, a simpler wording). Scripted pattern drills can prepare that. They do not replace it.

## Listening comprehension

Listening is taught, not only used as colour behind a flashcard. Learners hear Nepali they are ready to parse, answer a question about what was meant, and meet the same forms again in speech of their own. Comprehension and production stay linked.

## Personalised learning

The three audiences in [Audience](audience.md) do not start at the same lesson. Someone who understands dinner-table Nepali should not repeat the alphabet before they are allowed to speak. Someone who already speaks should be able to study the script against words they know. Personalisation is placement, pacing, and review of weak forms — not a different personality for the app each day.

## Native-speaker-validated content

Anything that teaches Nepali — explanations, example sentences, audio, corrections — is validated by native speakers before it is curriculum. Model output, community submissions, and draft lessons can feed the pipeline. They are not the curriculum until that review has happened. The rule is restated in [AI integration](../technical/ai-integration.md).

## Connected language reference

The [Grammar Library, Verb Conjugation Reference and Nepali Dictionary](language-reference.md) let learners move from a word to its forms, the explanation behind a construction and targeted practice. They share the reviewed content used in lessons and give learners a way to look up what they need during everyday use. These tools are part of the long-term product direction.

## What this excludes

Aafno does not compete on the size of a word list, on gamified hearts, or on an unstructured chatbot that “talks Nepali” with nobody accountable for the grammar. Those can exist beside the product later. They are not the difference.
