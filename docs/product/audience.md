# Audience

Aafno starts with three groups. They share a language and do not share a starting point. One linear beginner course would waste the first group, ignore the second, and under-teach the third.

## 1. Second-generation Nepalis

They grew up around spoken Nepali. They often catch the gist of family conversation, know the food, the festivals, and a scatter of words, and go quiet when someone expects a full answer.

What they usually need:

- A way to turn recognition into speech.
- Grammar that explains patterns they half-know (“why does this ending change?”).
- Sentence practice that starts from real replies, not from “hello, what is your name?”.
- Room to be beginners at speaking without being treated as beginners at the culture.
- Devanagari, if they cannot read, without pretending the spoken language is new to them.

What wastes their time: alphabet-first courses that delay speech, and phrase lists that never show how a sentence is built.

## 2. Speakers who need the script

They communicate in Nepali by voice. They cannot read or write Devanagari, or they read slowly enough that writing is not useful. Some learned Nepali at home in another country. Some went to school in another language. Some read a little and do not trust it.

What they usually need:

- A literacy path that assumes spoken competence.
- Sound-to-letter teaching that uses words they already say.
- Reading and writing practice on sentences, not only on isolated letters.
- Enough grammar in writing to see why a form is spelled the way it is spoken.

What wastes their time: a full oral beginner course, and a script course that only drills the alphabet chart.

## 3. Complete beginners

They have little or no Nepali. They may be partners, friends, researchers, or anyone who has decided to learn the language properly.

What they usually need:

- Sound, script, and meaning introduced in an order that does not overload any one of them.
- Explicit grammar from the first useful sentences.
- Listening that is slow enough to parse and real enough to be worth parsing.
- Speaking practice before the course has wandered into rare vocabulary.

What wastes their time: immersion with no structure, and translation drills that never ask them to say anything.

## Shared needs

All three groups need native-speaker-validated content, a clear explanation when a form changes, and feedback that is about the Nepali they produced. Personalisation is how Aafno respects the difference between these starts. It is not a separate product for each group. See [Curriculum](../learning/curriculum.md) for the pathways.

## Who is outside the first release

Dialect coaching, exam preparation, and children’s courses are not the initial brief. Content should still be careful: Standard Nepali as taught for general use, with honorifics treated as part of the language rather than a footnote. Later audiences can be added when the three paths above are real.
