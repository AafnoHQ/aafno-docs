# Language reference

Aafno’s agreed long-term reference tools help learners look up a word, understand a form, and practise using it without stepping through a course. The three tools draw on the same reviewed language content as lessons.

## Grammar Library

Search by construction, form, or communicative need, such as how to speak respectfully about a past event. Each entry can explain how a construction is formed, what it communicates and where it is appropriate. Include contextual examples, comparisons with related constructions, linked conjugation tables and targeted practice. Use the [grammar syllabus](../learning/grammar-syllabus.md) to organise entries and link recurring forms to their canonical concepts.

### Interactive pronoun reference

The proposed [pronoun reference](../roadmap/mvp.md#feature-backlog) connects first-, second- and third-person entries, singular and plural readings, validated register and household variants, case and possessive forms, contextual examples and approved audio. Link dictionary senses, canonical grammar and verb agreement in the Verb Conjugation Reference. Use the same three-language display policy. Household preferences can prioritise examples while reviewed usage and context govern correctness.

The proposed [progressive sentence builder](../roadmap/mvp.md#feature-backlog) reuses the sentence explorer’s canonical annotations. The explorer helps learners interpret existing sentences. The builder supports construction and controlled expansion, with separate evidence for guided and independent practice.

### Reference explorer extension

Extend the proposed sentence explorer and interactive pronoun reference with conversational speaker and listener switches, highlighted referents and tracking across turns. Compare visible and discourse-based demonstratives, self and mutual relationships, unspecified entities and linked relative references. When a scenario cannot distinguish candidates, offer the possible readings or allow a clarification request. This gives learners practice interpreting reference from evidence. Use the approved versions of G02-005, G02-006, G02-008, G02-010, G02-011 and G02-012 and reviewed dialogue with documented viewpoint, participants and ambiguity. Reuse shared examples and the independent Devanagari, Romanisation and English controls.

### Noun phrase builder extension

Extend the proposed progressive sentence builder with noun selection, explicit or contextual number, demonstratives, numerals, classifiers, quantities, and count and measurement units. Add eligible adjective and possessive combinations after reviewing their specifications. Compare alternatives in a situation and distinguish grammatical validity, naturalness, contextual suitability and meaning. Use reviewed combinations and canonical constraints from G03-004–008, G02-005, G06-010 and relevant G04, G06 and G15 specifications. Validate grouping, quantity, number and reference in context before supplying feedback. Use the same shared presentation controls.

Both extensions remain proposed capabilities for later product review.

### Adjective agreement explorer

Extend the proposed Grammar Library and contextual noun phrase builder so learners can compare supported variable and invariable forms, change a known referent’s relevant gender or number cues, inspect explicit or contextual plurality and compare attributive and predicative frames. Highlight what the adjective describes and explain why its form changes or stays constant. Keep class recognition separate from form selection and treat personal identity as distinct from grammatical cues. Use reviewed examples and constraints from G04-001–007 with dependencies on G03-004–006, G08 and G25; collect matched evidence for agreement, register and contextual alternatives before supporting combinations. Distinguish validity, naturalness and contextual fit, and show unresolved patterns as such.

### Progressive sentence modifier

Extend the proposed progressive sentence builder and sentence explorer to add time, place, frequency, manner, degree, duration, sequence and certainty to a clause. Highlight the modified expression, compare supported positions and explain changes in scope or ambiguity. Distinguish lexical adverbs from noun phrases or other expressions functioning adverbially. This helps learners interpret changes in a message before trying to produce them. Use reviewed relationships from G05-001–008 and G05-010 with dependencies on G01-005–006, G06, G09, G14, G16, G17 and G29. Validate attachment, prosody, naturalness and contextual meaning before exposing combinations. A supported form in isolation does not establish that it fits every other modifier or placement.

Both extensions reuse canonical content and independent Devanagari, Romanisation and English controls. They remain proposals for future review.

### Quantity and classifier builder

Extend the proposed contextual noun phrase builder and Grammar Library with a quantity or fraction, a referent and supported human and general counting forms. Compare object counts, measurement units and containers. Highlight the numeral, counting expression and noun. Compare reviewed noun or classifier omission, adjective and demonstrative modifiers, explicit and contextual plurality, and per-member distributions. This helps learners distinguish choice of counting form from assembly of the phrase.

For example, a draft progression is किताब / Kitaab / book → तीनवटा किताब / Tinwata kitaab / three books → तीनवटा नयाँ किताब / Tinwata nayaã kitaab / three new books → ती तीनवटा नयाँ किताब / Ti tinwata nayaã kitaab / those three new books. Review supported combinations and variants with G06-001, G06-003–010 and G06-012 and the G03 noun number and G04 agreement owners. Separate grammatical validity, naturalness and contextual fit. Restrict interactions to reviewed examples and combinations, with no unrestricted generation from independently listed components.

### Numerical time explorer

Extend the proposed Grammar Library with analogue and digital displays, conventional fractional and numerical minute readings, parts-of-day cues, duration and boundary timelines, and fractional quantity diagrams. Compare a draft clock reading साढे पाँच / Saadhe paanch / half past five with साढे पाँच घण्टा / Saadhe paanch ghantaa / five and a half hours as a duration candidate. Learners can practise interpreting a schedule or supplying a Nepali expression from a displayed value without being required to solve elapsed-time arithmetic. Hide the language model before production responses.

Use G06-001, G06-003 and G06-011, with G05 temporal and duration interpretation, G09 and G14 retained as dependencies. Review quarter-hour and numerical readings, omission and register, boundaries, pronunciation and any future audio before release. Both proposed extensions reuse canonical examples and independent Devanagari, Romanisation and English controls.

## Verb Conjugation Reference

Search by lemma or inflected form. Let learners explore forms by pronoun, person, number and gender where relevant, tense and aspect, honorific level or register, and positive or negative polarity. Identify irregular forms and show validated contextual examples. Link forms to their dictionary entries, relevant grammar explanations and practice. Explain the construction in Nepali usage terms with approximate English glosses.

Include a selectable [Highest honorific (Ati Uchcha Aadar)](../learning/highest-honorific.md) register with reviewed full and contracted forms shown as variants within that register wherever supported. Investigate every applicable verb and construction across tense and aspect, negation, questions, imperatives, modality, compounds, complex sentences and evidentiality. Show contextual usage tags such as family use, validated examples and audio from speakers who naturally use the register. Use reviewed variants in assessment when the prompt context permits them. Keep unverified forms as unspecified research gaps. See the [concept framework](../learning/grammar-concept-framework.md#highest-honorific-across-conjugations) and [Content QA](../learning/content-qa.md).

## Nepali Dictionary

Support bidirectional English–Nepali lookup and searches in English, Romanised Nepali and Devanagari. Use exact matches, reviewed aliases and fuzzy matching for spelling variation and mistyped queries. A search for *ramro*, *raamro* or राम्रो can surface the same entry. An inflected lookup such as *garchhu* can lead to गर्नु and explain the matched form. Rank plausible alternatives when the spelling, form or sense is ambiguous and show what each result matched.

An entry can include a headword, part of speech, definitions for multiple senses, translations, pronunciation and native-speaker audio where available, contextual examples, and register notes. Connect synonyms, antonyms, collocations and derived words by sense where appropriate. Link relevant grammar and conjugation entries. Learners can save words and add entries or individual senses to personal flashcards.

All three reference tools follow the [language presentation policy](../learning/curriculum.md#script-and-romanisation-display). Display preferences leave supported lookup inputs available.

## Shared language content

The tools, lessons, exercises, flashcards and eventual approved AI role-play draw on one validated knowledge base. Shared content includes lexical entries and senses, grammar concepts, forms, contextual examples and audio. Reviewed versions, provenance and register notes travel with reused content. Role-play delivery remains subject to the approval described in [AI integration](../technical/ai-integration.md).

An illustrative journey starts with गर्नु in the dictionary. The learner opens its conjugation reference, selects a respectful past construction, follows its grammar explanation, practises in context, and saves the verb to a personal flashcard. Lookup and saving are exposure or study actions. Mastery requires skill-specific evidence under the [curriculum structure](../learning/curriculum-structure.md).

### Shared variants and evidence

| Content entity | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar concept | Owns the canonical grammatical explanation. |
| Lexical entry | Owns the underlying word or lexical sense. |
| Language form | Represents a particular written or spoken form, including an inflection. |
| Variant relationship | Records how alternatives correspond, including variants within one register. |
| Usage evidence | Records contextual attestation, source, audio and documented distribution. |
| Review record | Records reviewer decisions and approved versions. |
| Learner preference | Guides example and practice selection. |

Grammar Library entries, dictionary senses and conjugation results can surface multiple validated alternatives with meanings, usage notes, register and audio. Link contextual examples to lexical senses, inflected forms, canonical explanations, variants and review evidence. Version canonical content and identify dependent assets for review when a shared version changes.

Distinguish speaker reports, recorded contextual attestations, linguistic review and approved content versions. Approve narrow household usage against appropriately reviewed household evidence. Wider claims require wider evidence. Use the [pilot backlog](../roadmap/mvp.md#feature-backlog) to track learner-facing capabilities.

## Licensing and publication

Use licensed lexical sources or original reviewed entries. Record source and reuse rights for definitions, examples, translations and audio. Unlicensed scraping and publication of unvalidated AI output are excluded. Apply [Content QA](../learning/content-qa.md) to every learner-facing version. The lookup examples and form labels on this page are illustrative drafts pending native-speaker and linguistic expert validation.
