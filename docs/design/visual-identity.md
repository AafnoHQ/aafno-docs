# Visual identity

Aafno’s brand direction is refined Nepali modernism: contemporary, warm, and culturally rooted. The work draws on Nepali architecture, traditional textiles, patterns, art, and Devanagari typography. Culture is the structure of the identity. It is not a sticker set applied to a generic language app.

The name and the lines are part of the identity, not campaign copy that can be swapped out:

> Aafno · आफ्नो

> Your language. Your roots. Your own.

> आफ्नो भाषा, आफ्नै पहिचान।  
> Your language, your identity.

आफ्नो means one’s own. The product message follows from the name: reconnect with the language that’s yours. The wordmark uses the Latin name **Aafno** with **आफ्नो** beside it. Neither is a translation footnote. In Nepali-first moments, आफ्नो can lead.

## What the references are for

These sources inform proportion, rhythm, material, and lettering. They are not costumes for the interface.

**Architecture.** Brick, timber, carved windows, and deep eaves suggest weight, shadow, and frames. Layout can use strong borders, layered planes, and a clear threshold between sections — the way a doorway separates inside from street. Ornament sits on structure. It does not cover it.

**Textiles.** Dhaka and other woven patterns contribute rhythm: repeat, stripe, and a small set of motifs used with discipline. A pattern can mark a section, a pathway, or a divider. A full-bleed textile behind body text fails both readability and the “refined” part of the brief.

**Art.** Traditional painting offers a warm ground, flat areas of colour, and careful figures. The interface stays flatter and quieter than a miniature. Illustration, when it exists, should look considered and specific, not like stock “Himalayan” clip art.

**Devanagari.** The script is a design material. Headlines, the wordmark, and lesson titles should be set in a Devanagari face that can carry real Nepali, including conjuncts, at the sizes we actually use. Latin and Devanagari need to sit in the same layout without one looking like a fallback font. If a display face cannot set body lesson text correctly, it is limited to short titles, and a text face does the teaching.

## Voice

Warm, exact, and adult. The product speaks to someone who is capable and currently missing a piece of the language. Heritage learners are not addressed as tourists in their own family language. Beginners are not addressed as children unless we later build a children’s product.

Nepali in the interface should be real Nepali, reviewed like lesson copy. Decorative Devanagari that is misspelled or meaningless is a product bug.

## Colour and type

A locked palette and a licensed font list are not decided yet. Direction, until a design pass fixes them:

- Grounds that feel like paper, limewashed wall, and brick — warm, not cold grey-on-white as the whole system.
- A deep dyed accent in the family of indigo or madder, used sparingly, the way a textile uses a strong thread.
- A metal or marigold highlight only for small marks, not for large fills.
- One Devanagari text family proven on lesson sentences, and one Latin family for English UI and glosses. Pair them for rhythm and weight, then stop adding faces.

When hex values and font files are chosen, they should be recorded here with the licence for each font. Do not treat this paragraph as the palette.

## Interface implications

- Lesson Nepali is the most important text on a learning screen. Chrome gets out of its way.
- English supports; it does not crowd the sentence being learned.
- Patterns and rules from architecture and cloth appear at the edges: rules, dividers, empty states, pathway markers.
- Motion, if used, is slow and structural (a panel opening, a step advancing). Nothing loops for entertainment behind a sentence.
- Script-learning screens need large, correct glyphs and generous space. Decoration that competes with a conjunct is removed.

## Marks we will not lean on

Prayer flags, map silhouettes, and mountain outlines are the default clip-art for anything Nepali. They can appear when a lesson is actually about them. They are not the logo system and not the background of the product. A flag emoji is not a brand colour.

## Accessibility

Warmth does not outrank reading. Text and controls meet ordinary contrast expectations on the final palette. Devanagari is never set so small, or in a face so thin, that vowel signs disappear. Audio in lessons has a visible way to replay. Colour is not the only signal of correct or incorrect.

## Status

This page is direction for the first design pass. It becomes a specification when logo files, colours, and type licences exist. Until then, product UI should prefer restraint that matches this page over a temporary theme we would have to unteach.
