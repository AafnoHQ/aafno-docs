# Grammar concept framework

Phase 2A.2 step 1 defines the reusable specification for an independently teachable and assessable grammar concept. The [grammar syllabus](grammar-syllabus.md) records category coverage. The [grammar catalogue](grammar-catalogue/index.md) contains 500 planned learner targets across 31 categories in Step 2. The [reconciliation records](grammar-catalogue/reconciliation.md) preserve all 527 original IDs and outcomes. The [Step 3A pilots](concepts/index.md#step-3a-pilots) demonstrate the framework. Use the [authoring guide](specification-authoring.md) for Step 3B batches. Linguistic and native-speaker validation is pending. Concept specifications explain what to teach and what evidence to look for. Phase 3 authors the actual lessons, activities and review items from reviewed specifications.

Give each distinct learning target one canonical concept identity. Verb and pronoun combinations are forms or examples within a concept. When another category needs the same concept, link to it. Specify a separate extension only if it adds its own teachable and assessable outcome, and link back to the canonical concept. Use [Present habitual](concepts/present-habitual.md) as the worked example and the [curriculum roadmap](curriculum-roadmap.md) for the review sequence.

## Reusable specification

Complete all 15 sections. Use **N/A** with a reason where a field genuinely does not apply. Record unknown or unvalidated information as a research gap.

| Section | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Concept identity | Catalogue ID and outcome, English title, proposed Nepali terminology and Romanisation, category, type, specification and validation status, and CEFR assignment (unassigned pending mapping). |
| 2. Learning outcomes | Observable things learners can understand, say, write or do in interaction. |
| 3. Meaning and usage | Communicative function, conditions for use and limits of approximate English labels. |
| 4. Formation rules | Components, order, stem or auxiliary changes, applicable polarity and relevant exceptions. |
| 5. Variations and agreement | Relevant person, gender, number and honorific distinctions, including Highest honorific (Ati Uchcha Aadar) and contextual family usage, along with attested alternatives and research gaps. |
| 6. Contextual examples | Devanagari, Romanisation and English for each model, with context and approved audio where available. |
| 7. Related constructions and contrasts | Canonical links, overlapping meanings and cues for choosing between constructions. |
| 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback | Likely errors, the underlying misunderstanding, an explanation and a correction or retry cue. |
| 9. Natural usage | How the construction appears in conversation, including omission, register choice, usage variation and context where relevant. |
| 10. Prerequisites | Required, helpful and co-taught knowledge by outcome and depth, plus extensions. |
| 11. Teaching depth | Recognition, scaffolded use, independent use and flexible use as appropriate to the outcomes. |
| 12. Activity types | Suitable input, response mode, scaffolding and practice formats. Examples here illustrate design requirements. |
| 13. Mastery evidence | What counts as evidence by outcome, depth and skill, including how to evaluate acceptable alternatives. |
| 14. Reference integration | Links to shared forms, lexical senses, examples and audio, and where each asset can be reused. |
| 15. Content validation | Sources and reuse rights, accepted variants, version, status and reviewer decisions. Apply the review checklist before publication. |

## Specification conventions

1. **Identifiable learning outcomes.** Number local observable outcomes LO1, LO2 and so on. Use them to connect activities, depth and evidence. Local outcomes belong to the existing concept ID.
2. **Canonical rule ownership.** Identify the owners of morphology, syntax, meaning, pragmatic functions, participant relationships, register and contextual variation. Cross-reference the owner. Shared examples and activities may elicit evidence for multiple concepts when each target is independently demonstrated.
3. **Contextual example metadata.** Apply the record below to examples, contrasts, dialogues and corrections.
4. **Corrective feedback.** Diagnose the misunderstanding, explain the relevant distinction, cue a meaningful retry and supply a correction where needed. Use the [assessment policy](proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for alternatives, register and unknown forms.
5. **Outcome-by-depth mapping.** Map outcomes to introduction, guided application, independent application and flexible application. Describe the changing cues, complexity and contexts. Record receptive and productive evidence separately.
6. **Structured validation register.** List individual open questions on terminology, classification, formation, agreement, meaning, naturalness, register, context, variation, spelling, Romanisation, glosses, audio, accepted alternatives, sources and reuse rights. Record evidence, review decisions and the affected version for each applicable question.
7. **Language presentation.** Ordinary learning and reference show Devanagari, Romanisation and English by default with [independent, persistent controls](curriculum.md#script-and-romanisation-display). Assessment may restrict a representation when relevant to the target skill. Romanisation remains provisional.

Apply these conventions to morphology, syntax, semantics, pragmatics, honorifics, case, participant structure, evidentiality and variation. Formation can describe conditions and relationships where a single rule or a conjugation table would misrepresent usage.

### Contextual example metadata

Record applicable Devanagari, Romanisation and English, intended meaning, conversational context, register, polarity, tense and aspect, participants, agreement, canonical owner, variant relationships, source or attestation, audio and validation status. Log missing representations as research gaps. Record understood referents and contextual clues for omission, roles and agreement controllers for experiencers, speaker and information source for hearsay, and attestation scope and distribution for household forms. Use only fields that apply.

## Highest honorific across conjugations {#highest-honorific-across-conjugations}

<span id="family-honorifics-across-conjugations"></span>

[Highest honorific (Ati Uchcha Aadar)](highest-honorific.md) documents the working terminology, guide claims and user-attested forms. Investigate every applicable verb and construction across tense and aspect, negation, questions, imperatives, modality, compounds, complex sentences and evidentiality. Use the existing [verb research matrix](grammar-catalogue/verbs-and-conjugation.md#step-3-family-honorific-research-matrix), [negation research](grammar-catalogue/relationships-and-transformations.md#step-3-negative-constructions-research), and [G25–G31 coverage](grammar-catalogue/honorifics-naturalness-and-advanced-language.md). Each applicable specification records the evidence, usage context and whether full and contracted variants are attested.

| Illustrative context | Devanagari | Romanisation | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard respectful | तपाईं सन्चै हुनुहुन्छ? | Tapaai sanchai hunnuhunchha? | Are you well? |
| Highest honorific (reported family usage) | हजुर सन्चै होइसिन्छ? | Hajur sanchai hoisinchha? | Are you well? |

Both examples await linguistic and native-speaker validation. Ask native speakers who naturally use the register to review grammar, naturalness, pronunciation and the relationships and settings where each form fits. Usage can vary within families and communities. Avoid attributing usage universally to a caste or community. Where an applicable equivalent is unknown or unvalidated, record a research gap and leave the form unspecified.

Make the highest honorific selectable in the [Verb Conjugation Reference](../product/language-reference.md#verb-conjugation-reference). Distinguish reviewed full and contracted variants. Link contextually accepted assessment variants and validated native audio to the same evidence. Apply the same review to form notes and corrective feedback.

## Prerequisites and shared content

Record a prerequisite as **required** when the target evidence depends on it, **helpful** when it supports practice, or **co-taught** when it can be developed alongside the outcome. Record extensions as follow-on applications. Revisit these decisions by depth during formal dependency mapping in Phase 2A.4. Oral learning and assessment must remain accessible without Devanagari literacy.

Use shared, reviewed versions of grammar concepts, forms, lexical senses, contextual examples and audio across the Grammar Library, Verb Conjugation Reference, Nepali Dictionary, lessons, flashcards and eventual approved AI role-play. Carry review status, provenance and register notes with the assets. A change creates a version and returns affected content to review. Looking up, saving or hearing an item can record exposure. Use the [teaching depth and evidence guidance](grammar-teaching-depth.md) to determine what demonstrates learning.

## Validation before publication

Review classification and terminology, formation and conjugation, spelling, Romanisation, glosses, naturalness, register and variation, accepted alternatives, explanatory feedback, audio and pronunciation, and sources and reuse rights. Record unresolved questions, version and reviewer decisions under [Content QA](content-qa.md). Publish only approved versions of learner-facing assets.
