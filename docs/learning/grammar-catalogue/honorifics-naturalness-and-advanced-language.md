## G25. Honorifics and social register {#g25}

Coverage · [G25 in the grammar syllabus](../grammar-syllabus.md#g25)

However, knowing how to form an honorific construction is different from knowing when, why and with whom to use it.

These are illustrative register choices, not a universal hierarchy. Usage depends on relationships, families and communities.

### Highest honorific in family speech

Family speech is one context for [Highest honorific (Ati Uchcha Aadar)](../highest-honorific.md).

The working examples आइस्यो and होइसिन्छ reflect reported family speech. Their draft spellings and classifications need review.

Teach reviewed variants with the relationships and settings in which speakers use them.

Usage varies across households and communities.

### Honorific vocabulary

Honorifics can also involve vocabulary selection rather than simply changing verb endings.

| Common form | Respectful alternative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| खानु — Khaanu | खानु / ज्युनार गर्नु | To eat |
| बस्नु — Basnu | बस्नु / विराजमान हुनु | To sit / be seated |
| भन्नु — Bhannu | भन्नु / आज्ञा गर्नु | To say / give an instruction |

These alternatives are not necessarily interchangeable. Some are highly formal, traditional or restricted to particular contexts. Their contemporary usage requires careful review.

## G26. Particles and discourse markers {#g26}

Coverage · [G26 in the grammar syllabus](../grammar-syllabus.md#g26)

This is arguably one of the most distinctive aspects of natural spoken Nepali.

Words such as नि, त, पो, है and नै can completely change the feeling, emphasis or implication of a sentence without significantly changing its literal proposition.

They are also particularly difficult to learn through direct English translation.

### Particle placement and scope

The same particle can affect different elements depending on its placement.

| Nepali | Romanisation | Illustrative meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| म पनि चिया पिउँछु। | Ma pani chiyaa piunchhu. | I also drink tea. |
| म चिया पनि पिउँछु। | Ma chiyaa pani piunchhu. | I drink tea as well as other things. |
| म मात्रै चिया पिउँछु। | Ma maatrai chiyaa piunchhu. | Only I drink tea. |
| म चिया मात्रै पिउँछु। | Ma chiyaa maatrai piunchhu. | I drink only tea. |

This demonstrates why particles need structured grammatical teaching rather than simply being included in vocabulary lists.

### The importance of native audio

For G26, native audio is especially important. A written sentence cannot always adequately convey the difference between:

- Seeking confirmation.
- Expressing disbelief.
- Making a playful correction.
- Reminding someone of shared knowledge.
- Softening a request.

Eventually, the same written construction could have multiple validated audio examples demonstrating different contextual meanings.

## G27. Evidentiality, inference and discovery {#g27}

Coverage · [G27 in the grammar syllabus](../grammar-syllabus.md#g27)

Nepali has grammatical constructions that allow speakers to communicate not only what they know, but how they came to know it.

For example, छ, रहेछ, छ रे and होला communicate different relationships to information.

English can express these distinctions, but often requires additional words or phrases.

### Four different relationships to the same information

How do you know?  
Unmarked assertion  
You state that he is at home without explicitly marking how you know.  
ऊ घरमा छ।

U gharma chha.

He's at home.

An important distinction is that छ does not necessarily mean the speaker witnessed something firsthand. It is an unmarked assertion, unlike constructions that explicitly indicate reported information or discovery.

### Event discovery

| Nepali | Romanisation | Illustrative meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| उसले खाना खायो। | Usle khaana khaayo. | He ate food. |
| उसले खाना खाएछ। | Usle khaana khaae chha. | It turns out he ate food. |
| ऊ गएछ। | U gaechha. | It turns out he has gone. |
| ऊ आएको रहेछ। | U aaeko rahechha. | It turns out he had come. |
| ऊ आएको रहेनछ। | U aaeko rahenachha. | It turns out he hadn't come. |

The spelling, Romanisation, temporal interpretation and distribution of these constructions will require detailed review.

### Teaching discovery through situations

Rather than simply asking learners to memorise रहेछ, we could present situations.

Situation 1

You look outside and discover that it has rained.

पानी परेछ।  
Paani parechha.

Oh, it has rained.

Situation 2

A friend tells you that it rained earlier.

पानी पर्‍यो रे।  
Paani paryo re.

I hear it rained.

Situation 3

You see wet ground and infer that it probably rained.

पानी पर्‍यो होला।  
Paani paryo holaa.

It probably rained.

These are illustrative contexts, not mutually exclusive rules. Natural Nepali may permit several constructions depending on the speaker's perspective.

### Textbook versus conversational Nepali

Consider this exchange.

Fully explicit

तिमीले खाना खायौ?

Timile khaana khaayau?

Did you eat?

हो, मैले खाना खाएँ।

Ho, maile khaana khaaẽ.

Yes, I ate food.

Contextually reduced

खाना खायौ?

Khaana khaayau?

Did you eat?

खाएँ।

Khaaẽ.

I did.

Both exchanges can be grammatically appropriate. The second simply relies more heavily on shared context.

This is particularly important for heritage learners who may already understand reduced forms intuitively but struggle to construct them independently.

## G31. Language variation and register {#g31}

Coverage · [G31 in the grammar syllabus](../grammar-syllabus.md#g31)

Our final grammar category addresses an important reality: Nepali is not a single, completely uniform set of grammatical rules used identically by every speaker.

People speak differently depending on their family, region, community, generation, social environment and communicative purpose.

Aafno should teach a reliable common foundation while helping learners understand legitimate variation.

### Spoken versus formal language

| Everyday construction | More formal alternative | Intended meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| किनभने | किनकि | Because |
| तर | तथापि | But / however |
| त्यसैले | अतः | Therefore |
| धेरै | अत्यधिक | A lot / excessive degree |
| बारेमा | सम्बन्धमा | About / concerning |

These are contextual comparisons, not exact interchangeable synonyms. For example, अत्यधिक often expresses a stronger degree than धेरै, and तथापि typically introduces a concessive contrast.

The purpose is to develop awareness of register, not mechanically replace ordinary words with more formal vocabulary.

### Code-switching

For heritage learners living outside Nepal, code-switching is often part of everyday family conversation.

Consider:

म भोलि अफिस जान्छु।

Ma bholi office jaanchhu.

I'll go to the office tomorrow.

मैले त्यो मिटिङ क्यान्सल गरेँ।

Maile tyo meeting cancel garẽ.

I cancelled that meeting.

Even where English lexical items are incorporated, the surrounding sentence can retain Nepali grammatical structures.

This creates useful opportunities to teach sentence construction using familiar vocabulary, before gradually expanding the learner's Nepali lexical knowledge.

## Illustrative assessment experience

Question 1 of 7  
G25 · Honorifics

Which is a standard respectful way of asking whether someone is well?

A.

तँ सन्चै छस्?  
Tã sanchai chhas?

B.

तिमी सन्चै छौ?  
Timi sanchai chhau?

C.

तपाईं सन्चै हुनुहुन्छ?  
Tapaai sanchai hunuhunchha?

D.

ऊ सन्चै छ।  
U sanchai chha.

**Correct answer: C**

तपाईं and हुनुहुन्छ are standard respectful forms for asking whether someone is well.

### Question 2 of 7 — G26 · Particles

**Which sentence means "I drink only tea"?**

A. म पनि चिया पिउँछु।  
*Ma pani chiyaa piunchhu.*

B. म मात्रै चिया पिउँछु।  
*Ma maatrai chiyaa piunchhu.*

C. म चिया मात्रै पिउँछु।  
*Ma chiyaa maatrai piunchhu.*

D. म चिया पनि पिउँछु।  
*Ma chiyaa pani piunchhu.*

**Correct answer: C**

Explanation: मात्रै follows चिया, restricting the object rather than the person.

### Question 3 of 7 — G27 · Discovery

**You have just discovered that someone is at home. Which sentence explicitly expresses discovery?**

A. ऊ घरमा छ।  
*U gharma chha.*

B. ऊ घरमा रहेछ।  
*U gharma rahechha.*

C. ऊ घरमा छ रे।  
*U gharma chha re.*

D. ऊ घरमा होला।  
*U gharma holaa.*

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: रहेछ can communicate newly discovered or newly recognised information.

### Question 4 of 7 — G28 · Morphology

**Which word means "writer"?**

A. लेख्नु — *Lekhnu*

B. लेखन — *Lekhan*

C. लेखक — *Lekhak*

D. लेखाइ — *Lekhaai*

**Correct answer: C**

Explanation: लेखक is an established noun meaning writer or author.

### Question 5 of 7 — G29 · Conversational grammar

**Someone asks खाना खायौ? Which is a natural affirmative response without repeating unnecessary information?**

A. खाएँ। — *Khaaẽ.*

B. खान्छौ। — *Khaanchhau.*

C. खानु। — *Khaanu.*

D. खाएको मान्छे। — *Khaaeko maanchhe.*

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: The subject and object can be recovered from context, so the conjugated verb can be sufficient.

### Question 6 of 7 — G30 · Figurative language

**What does नाच्न नजान्ने आँगन टेढो generally communicate?**

A. Learning to dance takes time.

B. A courtyard needs repairing.

C. Someone blames circumstances for their own shortcomings.

D. Dancing is an important tradition.

**Correct answer: C**

Explanation: The proverb uses a figurative situation to describe misplaced blame.

### Question 7 of 7 — G31 · Language variation

**A learner uses a validated highest-honorific form instead of a standard respectful form. How should Aafno respond?**

A. Always mark it incorrect.

B. Accept it where appropriate to the task and context.

C. Automatically accept every unfamiliar form.

D. Remove family honorifics from the exercise.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: Validated alternatives should be accepted where they satisfy the target and contextual requirements.

Questions 1–6 illustrate recognition and guided application. Question 7 illustrates Aafno's assessment policy. It does not directly measure Nepali language proficiency.

All seven questions, answer keys and explanations are draft illustrations pending linguistic and native-speaker validation.

### Independent and flexible application

At deeper teaching levels, we could assess learners through activities such as having them explain a recent family gathering, report what different relatives said, express something they discovered unexpectedly, and adapt their language when speaking to different people.

The objective would be to gather evidence of natural grammatical application rather than simply testing whether learners know the definitions of particles or honorifics.
