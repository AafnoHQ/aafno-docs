# Grammar syllabus

Phase 2A.1 records the agreed scope of Aafno’s master Nepali grammar inventory for beginner through advanced learning. It covers how sentences and words are formed, what constructions communicate, and when speakers use them. This matters especially for heritage learners who may recognise forms they are still learning to produce.

**Status:** documented for review. All linguistic classifications, examples, English glosses, spelling, Romanisation, conjugations and claims about variation are drafts pending native-speaker and linguistic expert validation. English tense and aspect labels are approximate signposts. Family and community usage varies by speaker, relationship and setting. Learner-facing material must pass [Content QA](content-qa.md).

G01–G31 are stable category IDs across five groupings. Learners can encounter a concept in several pathways and at several depths. The IDs organise coverage, with sequencing, prerequisites and integrated MVP selection scheduled in the [curriculum roadmap](curriculum-roadmap.md). Overlapping entries point to one canonical concept identity. The links below are coverage relationships. Individual concept specifications belong to Phase 2A.2 Step 3.

Phase 2A.1 product scope is approved and linguistic validation is pending. Phase 2A.2 step 1 defines the [concept framework](grammar-concept-framework.md) and [teaching depth](grammar-teaching-depth.md), with a [present habitual worked example](concepts/present-habitual.md). The Step 2 [grammar catalogue](grammar-catalogue/index.md) records agreed product coverage for [58 concepts in G01–G06](grammar-catalogue/grammar-foundations.md), [106 concepts in G07–G13](grammar-catalogue/verbs-and-conjugation.md), [101 concepts in G14–G18](grammar-catalogue/relationships-and-transformations.md) and [114 concepts in G19–G24](grammar-catalogue/connecting-ideas.md). This totals 379 agreed concepts across 24 of 31 categories (77.4% category coverage). G25–G31 are next, with 7 categories remaining. Concept IDs remain provisional until full catalogue reconciliation, and linguistic and native-speaker validation is pending.

Apply the [family honorific requirement](grammar-concept-framework.md#family-honorifics-across-conjugations) to every applicable conjugation, including habitual, progressive, past, future, perfect, negation, questions, imperatives, modality and compound verbs. Document reviewed family forms, contextual usage, validated native audio and accepted assessment variants. Record a research gap wherever an equivalent is unknown or unvalidated. [G25](#g25) explains register choice across the syllabus.

For example, a learner should understand how a basic sentence can gradually become more expressive.

**म जान्छु।**

*Ma jaanchhu.*

I go.

**म घर जान्छु।**

*Ma ghar jaanchhu.*

I go home.

**म हरेक दिन घर जान्छु।**

*Ma harek din ghar jaanchhu.*

I go home every day.

**म हरेक दिन काम सकेर घर जान्छु।**

*Ma harek din kaam sakera ghar jaanchhu.*

I go home every day after finishing work.

This is the type of sentence-building ability we want Aafno to develop.

### G09. Tense and aspect — काल र पक्ष {#g09}

I know this is an area you particularly wanted Aafno to cover comprehensively.

Rather than simply teaching the traditional twelve English tenses, we should cover the Nepali constructions used to communicate those meanings, including where the two languages differ.

Using गर्नु (to do) as an illustrative verb:

| Construction or meaning | Example | Approximate English |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Present habitual | म गर्छु | I do |
| Present progressive | म गर्दै छु | I am doing |
| Present perfect | मैले गरेको छु | I have done |
| Continuing activity | म गर्दै आएको छु | I have been doing |
| Simple past | मैले गरेँ | I did |
| Past habitual | म गर्थेँ | I used to do |
| Past progressive | म गर्दै थिएँ | I was doing |
| Past perfect | मैले गरेको थिएँ | I had done |
| Continuing past activity | म गर्दै आएको थिएँ | I had been doing |
| Future | म गर्नेछु | I will do |
| Future progressive | म गर्दै हुनेछु | I will be doing |
| Future completion | मैले गरिसकेको हुनेछु | I will have finished doing |
| Immediate intention | म गर्न लागेको छु | I am about to do |
| Completed action | मैले गरिसकेँ | I have finished doing |
| Experienced action | मैले गरेको छु | I have done it before (in context) |
| Repeated action | म गरिरहन्छु | I keep doing |
| Newly discovered information | उसले गरेछ | It turns out they did it |

These are illustrative mappings, not a claim that each English tense has an exact Nepali equivalent.

Beyond the individual constructions, the syllabus should cover:

- Habitual versus ongoing actions.
- Completed versus incomplete actions.
- Temporary versus permanent states.
- Actions that started in the past and continue now.
- Past routines and discontinued habits.
- Future intentions, predictions and plans.
- Actions occurring before or after another action.
- Evidentiality, discovery and inference.
- Time expressions and their relationship with verb forms.
- Switching between constructions within conversation.

This will allow us to create tense recognition, conjugation, sentence transformation and comparison exercises later.

### G10. Copulas, existence and identity {#g10}

Nepali has several ways of expressing what English broadly communicates through the verb *to be*\.

| Subtopic | Coverage |
|--------|--------|
| Identity | हो |
| State and condition | छ |
| Existence | छ, छन् |
| Infinitive and auxiliary | हुनु |
| Negative identity | होइन |
| Negative existence or state | छैन |
| Past identity | थियो and related forms |
| Past states | थिएँ, थियो and related forms |
| Future states | हुनेछ and other constructions |
| Honorific forms | हुनुहुन्छ |
| Location | Expressing where something is |
| Possession | Expressing what someone has |
| Becoming and happening | Different uses of हुनु |
| Discovery and inference | रहेछ |

**यो किताब हो।**

*Yo kitaab ho.*

This is a book. — Identity

**किताब टेबलमा छ।**

*Kitaab tablema chha.*

The book is on the table. — Location

**मसँग किताब छ।**

*Masanga kitaab chha.*

I have a book. — Possession

**यो किताब रहेछ।**

*Yo kitaab rahechha.*

Oh, it turns out this is a book. — Discovery

## Part 5 — Honorifics, naturalness and advanced language

### G25. Honorifics and social register {#g25}

Teach the full grammatical system of honorifics, including how speakers address listeners and refer to other people. Address choices depend on relationships, community, context and individual preferences.

| Address | Romanisation | Register | Illustrative contexts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| तँ | Ta | Intimate or low honorific | Close relationships and some family contexts. It can be disrespectful in other contexts. |
| तिमी | Timi | Familiar | Friends, peers, younger people and familiar relationships. |
| तपाईं | Tapaai | Respectful | Polite interaction with elders or strangers and in professional contexts. |
| हजुर | Hajur | Respectful address | Respectful interaction, including many family settings. |

| Subtopic | Coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Second-person honorifics | तँ, तिमी, तपाईं, हजुर |
| Third-person honorifics | ऊ, उनी, उहाँ |
| Honorific verb conjugation | Different endings and constructions |
| Honorific imperatives | Commands and requests |
| Honorific questions | Asking respectfully |
| Honorific negation | Negative respectful constructions |
| Honorific vocabulary | Alternative respectful verbs and expressions |
| Family relationships | Parents, grandparents, relatives |
| Age and familiarity | Choosing socially appropriate forms |
| Professional register | Workplace and formal interactions |
| Family-specific conjugations | Forms such as आइस्यो and होइसिन्छ |
| Regional and community variation | Differences in actual usage |
| Register switching | Adapting speech to different people |
| Pragmatic meaning | How respect, affection and distance are communicated |
| Social mistakes and repair | Recovering from inappropriate register |

| Context | Nepali | Romanisation | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard respectful | तपाईं सन्चै हुनुहुन्छ? | Tapaai sanchai hunnuhunchha? | Are you well? |
| Family usage variant | हजुर सन्चै होइसिन्छ? | Hajur sanchai hoisinchha? | Are you well? |

Validate the spelling, conjugation patterns, usage and distribution of both examples. Family forms may vary within a community. Explain the social context in which each form is used and evaluate it in that context. In request practice, compare how agreement and softening change the force of a request. Link pronouns in [G02](#g02), agreement in [G08](#g08), requests in [G12](#g12), negation in [G16](#g16), questions in [G17](#g17) and variation in [G31](#g31).

Apply the [family honorific requirement](grammar-concept-framework.md#family-honorifics-across-conjugations) whenever another category teaches or assesses a conjugation.

### G27. Evidentiality, inference and discovery {#g27}

| Subtopic | Coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Direct knowledge | Personally known information |
| Newly discovered information | रहेछ |
| Discovery-related verb forms | गएछ, गरेछ |
| Reported information | रे |
| Inferred information | होला |
| Assumptions | Expressing what is presumed |
| Surprise | Discovering unexpected facts |
| Degrees of certainty | Certain, probable, possible |
| Source of information | Heard, witnessed or inferred |

| Information source or stance | Nepali | Romanisation | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | ऊ घरमा छ। | U gharma chha. | They are at home. |
| Discovery | ऊ घरमा रहेछ। | U gharma rahechha. | It turns out they are at home. |
| Reported | ऊ घरमा छ रे। | U gharma chha re. | Apparently, they are at home — someone said so. |
| Inferred | ऊ घरमा होला। | U gharma hola. | They are probably at home. |

Heritage learners may recognise these distinctions and still need practice producing them. In narrative practice, compare how speakers introduce or react to newly learned information using examples such as उसले गरेछ, and how they express an inference with forms such as गरेको होला. Link modality in [G11](#g11), reported speech in [G23](#g23) and the discourse use of रे in [G26](#g26).

## Cross-category relationships

Use one concept across the inventory, the [Grammar Library](../product/language-reference.md#grammar-library) and the [Verb Conjugation Reference](../product/language-reference.md#verb-conjugation-reference). A category can introduce a form and link to its fuller treatment elsewhere. Reuse those concepts in explanations, flashcards, listening, sentence-building and AI roleplay.

| Shared coverage | Canonical treatment and related categories |
| --- | --- |
| Pronouns, agreement and honorifics | [G02](#g02) covers pronouns, [G08](#g08) covers agreement mechanics, and [G25](#g25) covers social choice with variation in [G31](#g31). |
| Time, auxiliary constructions and evidence | [G09](#g09) covers time and aspect, [G10](#g10) covers copular and existential uses, and [G27](#g27) covers evidential meaning, linked from modality [G11](#g11) and reported speech [G23](#g23). |
| Possession and reference | [G15](#g15) covers possessive constructions, including reflexive possession referenced from [G02](#g02). Link marker functions in [G14](#g14) and existential possession in [G10](#g10). |
| Comparison | [G18](#g18) covers comparison and degree. Link adjective, adverb and marker uses from [G04](#g04), [G05](#g05) and [G14](#g14). |
| Negative and interrogative forms | [G16](#g16) covers negation and [G17](#g17) covers questions. Link relevant verb, modal, request, embedded-clause and particle uses from [G08](#g08), [G11](#g11), [G12](#g12), [G22](#g22) and [G26](#g26). |
| Non-finite and conditional forms | [G20](#g20) covers shared non-finite forms. Link how they work in multi-verb constructions [G13](#g13), conditions [G21](#g21), subordinate clauses [G22](#g22) and word formation [G28](#g28). |
| Passive and causative constructions | [G24](#g24) covers voice and agency, linked from verb valency [G07](#g07), advanced verbs [G13](#g13) and case [G14](#g14). |
| Quantities and classifiers | [G06](#g06) covers classifier constructions, linked from countability and plurality in [G03](#g03). |
| Topic, focus and natural interaction | [G29](#g29) covers discourse-level information structure and conversational grammar, linked from sentence structure [G01](#g01), connectors [G19](#g19), particles [G26](#g26) and idiomatic use [G30](#g30). |

The five parts contain 6, 7, 5, 6 and 7 categories respectively, for 31 categories in all. Lessons draw on this inventory as needed rather than following 31 linear courses.

For example, a lesson about talking to a grandmother about tomorrow’s plans can bring together [G02](#g02) for choosing हजुर, [G08](#g08) for appropriate verb endings, [G09](#g09) for future plans, [G14](#g14) for destinations, [G17](#g17) for questions, [G25](#g25) for showing respect and [G26](#g26) for particles.
