# Methodology

Aafno teaches Nepali so that a learner can speak it, follow it, and read it. The method is the set of choices that make the [vision](../product/vision.md) and the [differences](../product/usps.md) concrete.

## Language before points

A lesson earns its place by giving the learner something they can say, hear, or read in a real exchange. Points, streaks, and badges may record effort. They do not decide what is taught next. What is taught next depends on what the learner can already do, and on which forms are still unreliable.

## Grammar in the sentence

A grammar point arrives inside sentences, with the rule stated in plain language. The learner then changes the sentence on purpose: a different person, a different honorific, a different time, a negated form. Recognition questions can check that the rule was noticed. Production is what shows that it was learned.

Explanations should be short enough to use during practice and precise enough that a native-speaker reviewer can agree they are right. Jargon is allowed when it prevents a wrong idea. It is not allowed as decoration.

## Construction, then variation

The usual order inside a pattern:

1. Hear and read a correct sentence in context.
2. See how it is built (slots, endings, word order).
3. Rebuild it.
4. Vary one slot at a time.
5. Use the pattern in a short exchange where the other line is not fully predictable.

Skipping to free conversation before step 4 produces guessing. Stopping at step 2 produces people who can explain Nepali and cannot speak it.

## Listening as parsing

Listening items ask what was meant, not only which word appeared. Early audio is clear and paced so the forms being taught are audible. Later audio can be more natural. A transcript is a study tool after an attempt, not a way to avoid listening.

New script learners are not required to read a transcript they cannot read yet. Audio and meaning come first for them; Devanagari is added when that strand says so. See [Curriculum](curriculum.md).

## Speech is practised aloud

Speaking tasks require spoken Nepali, not only typed answers. Feedback should point at a specific problem when one is known: a sound, an ending, an honorific, a missing word. Generic praise is not feedback. Until a speech model is licensed and checked, feedback can be human, rubric-based, or limited to forms the lesson already knows how to judge. See [AI integration](../technical/ai-integration.md).

## Literacy is a strand, not a gate

Devanagari is part of learning Nepali. It is not a tollbooth in front of speech for people who already understand the spoken language. Letters are taught with the sounds and words the learner can use, then with syllable structure, then with words and short sentences. Handwriting and typing are both in scope; the first release can emphasise reading and typing if handwriting tools are not ready. That limit belongs in the [MVP](../roadmap/mvp.md), not in the method.

## Personalisation without a new curriculum each time

There is one body of Nepali to learn and three ways in:

- Skip or compress what an assessment shows the learner can already do.
- Spend longer where production fails even if recognition is fine. This is the usual second-generation pattern.
- Open the script strand against known spoken vocabulary for literate-in-speech learners.
- Keep beginners on a fuller path that introduces sound, meaning, grammar, and script together, in a controlled order.

Review returns forms that were wrong or hesitant. It does not only return whatever was scheduled yesterday.

## Validation

A lesson is draft until a native speaker has checked the Nepali, the explanation, and the audio notes. Review looks for incorrect language, unnatural example sentences, missing honorific context, and explanations that would teach a false rule. Drafts can be generated or outlined with tools. Publication cannot. The release workflow and the required review record are in [Content QA](content-qa.md).

## Naturalness, register, and variation

Correct is not always the same as useful. Examples should identify the register they teach where it matters: everyday, respectful, or formal. Standard Nepali is the shared teaching baseline; regional, family, and code-switched expressions may be introduced as labelled variation, never as an unmarked universal rule. A lesson must not present one household's usage as the only natural way to speak.

## Tone of instruction

The voice of the product is warm and exact. It does not talk down to heritage speakers, and it does not romanticise the language instead of teaching it. Cultural context appears when it changes what you should say. It is not a substitute for the lesson.
