# Content QA

Content QA protects the learner from fluent-sounding but wrong Nepali. It applies to every learner-facing explanation, sentence, accepted answer, transcript, translation, audio asset, and model-assisted correction. It is the release gate for curriculum, not a retrospective tidy-up.

## Workflow

1. **Draft.** An author records the unit contract: outcome, target forms, examples, activities, marking rules, and any register notes. AI may help draft, but the draft is not learner-visible.
2. **Language review.** A native-speaker reviewer checks grammar, spelling, naturalness, honorific context, register, English glosses, and whether the explanation teaches a true rule.
3. **Activity review.** The reviewer confirms that prompts, accepted answers, and feedback actually test the unit outcome instead of rewarding recognition alone.
4. **Audio review.** For recorded or generated audio, a reviewer checks that the words, pronunciation, pace, and transcript match the approved item.
5. **Accept or return.** Accepted content is publishable. Anything changed after acceptance returns to review for the affected fields.

## Required record

Each accepted content item needs a durable record of its version, status, reviewer, review date, source or audio reference, and review notes. The application must only serve accepted versions in a learner path. Draft, returned, and retired content must stay out of normal lesson delivery.

## Review checklist

- Is the Nepali grammatical, natural, and appropriate to the stated register?
- Are honorifics, pronouns, verb forms, postpositions, and spelling consistent with the teaching intent?
- Does any explanation overstate a rule or hide a common exception that matters at this level?
- Is the English support accurate without replacing the Nepali task?
- Can the activity be completed using the target form rather than a memorised fixed line?
- Do the audio and transcript exactly match the approved text?

## Corrections in production

Reported content is triaged as either a typo, a teaching-risk issue, or a broader curriculum change. A teaching-risk issue is unpublished or disabled until reviewed. A correction keeps the original review history and records why the learner-facing version changed.

## Open decisions

- The reviewer roster, number of required approvals, and service-level target for review.
- The editorial guide for Romanised Nepali, regional variants, family expressions, and code-switching.
- The reporting route and learner-facing wording for content issues.
