# Proficiency framework

Aafno uses CEFR-inspired A1–C2 bands to make progress legible, while assessing Nepali skills independently. They are descriptive internal bands, not an accredited CEFR alignment, exam, or promise that a learner has the same ability in every skill.

## Skill model

| Type | Skills | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receptive | Listening, reading | Interpret meaning, intent, key detail, and relevant register from unfamiliar but level-appropriate input. |
| Productive | Speaking, writing | Create an understandable, context-appropriate response; adapt known patterns rather than repeat a fixed line. |
| Enabling | Grammar | Recognise and apply the forms needed by the band, including choosing between known alternatives in context. |

Grammar does not substitute for communication evidence. Likewise, receptive success alone does not award a productive level. Devanagari literacy is measured through reading and writing, and is independent of spoken fluency.

## Nepali-specific band descriptors

| Level | Listening (receptive) | Speaking (productive) | Grammar (enabling) | Reading (receptive) | Writing (productive) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A1** | Understands very familiar, clearly delivered words and short supported exchanges. | Gives rehearsed personal information and uses short guided responses. | Applies a small taught set of basic sentence patterns with support. | Decodes familiar Devanagari words and very short supported lines where literacy is active. | Types or writes familiar words and short guided sentences in Devanagari where literacy is active. |
| **A2** | Follows short everyday exchanges on familiar topics when pace and vocabulary are controlled. | Handles predictable everyday exchanges; can ask and answer simple follow-up questions. | Uses taught basic forms, negation, questions, and common relationships with partial independence. | Understands short practical messages and simple connected text on familiar topics. | Produces short connected messages using taught patterns; errors may remain but meaning is usually recoverable. |
| **B1** | Follows the main point and key details of clear, ordinary conversation on familiar and some less familiar matters. | Describes experiences, plans, needs, and opinions in connected speech; can repair a simple breakdown. | Selects among a growing range of taught forms and connects ideas, though control is uneven. | Understands the main points and useful detail in everyday connected texts. | Writes connected everyday messages or short narratives with an appropriate basic register. |
| **B2** | Understands extended natural conversation and many media-like inputs when topic and variety are reasonably familiar. | Sustains discussion, explains a viewpoint, and adjusts wording for common social situations. | Controls a broad working range of forms and can notice/repair many errors; increasingly manages nuance and register. | Reads a range of general-interest and practical texts, following argument and implied connections. | Produces clear, organised text for common practical, narrative, and explanatory purposes. |
| **C1** | Follows extended, fast, or less structured speech, including many implied meanings and shifts in register. | Speaks flexibly and fluently across personal, professional, and community contexts; manages sensitive register choices. | Uses complex structures and discourse choices with reliable control, consulting support for uncommon edge cases. | Reads substantial general, professional, and literary-adjacent texts with limited support. | Writes well-structured, audience-aware text and can revise for clarity, register, and effect. |
| **C2** | Understands virtually all intended meaning in demanding spoken input, including subtle stance, humour, and register where the variety is familiar. | Expresses precise meaning spontaneously and adapts style across demanding interactions. | Uses a very wide range of structures and choices precisely; remaining gaps are specialised or variety-specific. | Reads demanding texts with nuanced interpretation across registers and genres. | Produces polished, precise text across demanding purposes, adapting voice and convention to audience. |

These descriptors deliberately avoid prescribing a single 'correct' family or regional voice. At upper levels, variation awareness means recognising what a given speaker or context calls for, not erasing variation.

## Observable evidence

Placement and progression tasks should elicit evidence rather than rely only on self-rating:

- Listening: choose or state the meaning of an utterance; identify a relevant detail, request, or social cue.
- Speaking: answer a prompt, vary a known frame, ask a related question, and recover from a supported misunderstanding.
- Grammar: complete, transform, or choose a form in context, then use it in a new sentence.
- Reading: derive meaning from Devanagari appropriate to the learner's literacy band, not merely name characters.
- Writing: create a Devanagari response for a purpose; typing is valid evidence while handwriting capability is not yet a product requirement.

Tasks may offer Romanisation as learning support according to the learner setting. It must not make a reading result appear higher than the learner's Devanagari reading evidence.

## Placement and progression principles

1. Start with brief self-report plus a small sampled task set; report a recommendation, not a fixed identity.
2. Assign a provisional band per skill, with confidence/evidence recorded separately from the band.
3. Let learners override the recommendation and revise it after early-unit evidence.
4. Require prerequisite concepts for a target task, but permit another skill to progress independently.
5. Promote a band only with repeated evidence across relevant task types; a single correct recognition item is not enough.
6. Use review to distinguish lack of retrieval, comprehension, form control, and register choice before changing a pathway.

## Alignment limits and validation

**Proposed:** CEFR labels are a useful common language for goals. Aafno should describe them as “CEFR-inspired” until descriptors, tasks, and cut scores have been piloted; it does not claim official alignment.

**Requires native-speaker validation:** all final task prompts, benchmark answers, register judgments, audio, scoring annotations, and descriptors that reference naturalness or variation. Assessment accessibility and reliability require separate testing with the three starting profiles.

**Open decisions:** whether the MVP displays bands beyond A2/B1, the evidence threshold and reassessment cadence, how confidence is explained to learners, and whether handwriting becomes separately reported once supported.
