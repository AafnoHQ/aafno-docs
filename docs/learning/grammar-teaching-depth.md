# Grammar teaching depth

Depth describes what learners can do with a concept and under what conditions. A concept can be introduced early and revisited at greater depth. Depth is independent of CEFR-inspired placement. Placement can establish evidence without requiring lesson completion. Use this guidance with the [concept framework](grammar-concept-framework.md) and [worked example](concepts/present-habitual.md).

| Depth | Learner performance |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Recognise the form and identify what it means in a familiar example. |
| Guided application | Apply the construction to familiar examples with scaffolding such as sentence frames, cues or a worked model. |
| Independent application | Construct original sentences without a supplied target form or sentence frame, and use them to communicate in a familiar context. |
| Flexible application | Transfer the construction to unfamiliar contexts, select register and distinguish it from competing constructions while communicating. |

## Evidence

Track receptive comprehension and productive use separately. Track implicit ability to understand or use a construction and explicit ability to explain it separately as well. A learner can apply agreement in conversation without naming the rule, or describe the rule while still needing help to speak.

| Evidence | What to observe |
| --- | --- |
| Recognition | Identifies a form or distinguishes it from a distractor. |
| Comprehension | Interprets what a sentence or exchange means in context. |
| Guided production | Supplies or transforms forms with a scaffold. |
| Independent production | Generates a new utterance that communicates the intended meaning. |
| Transfer | Chooses an appropriate form in a new context or with an unfamiliar example. |
| Interaction | Responds to an unscripted turn and adapts or repairs as needed. |
| Retention | Demonstrates the relevant ability again after an interval. |

Use multiple-choice evidence for recognition or comprehension where suitable. Mastery needs evidence beyond multiple choice, including production where the outcome calls for it. Judge the target grammar and allow reviewed alternatives appropriate to the prompt's context and register, including family forms where applicable. Record the support provided so a scaffolded response and an independent response can be interpreted correctly. Lookup, saving, and lesson completion record exposure or participation. Phase 4 will define assessment and mastery rules, including any thresholds.

## Diagnostic dimensions

Assess meaning, basic conjugation, honorific agreement, negation, independent sentence construction and flexible use separately. For example, someone may recognise a habitual sentence and use a familiar first-person form while still needing support with respectful agreement or register switching. A diagnostic can then target the specific missing evidence. Any mock scores used to illustrate an interface or example are fictional and carry no placement or mastery decision.

## Highest honorific across teaching depths

Use reviewed [Highest honorific (Ati Uchcha Aadar)](highest-honorific.md) examples with supporting native audio. Recognition focuses on interpreting the form and its social context. Guided use asks learners to select or produce a reviewed form with context and prompts. Independent application asks learners to choose and sustain contextually appropriate forms in unfamiliar exchanges. Accept validated full or contracted variants when the prompt context permits them and the response demonstrates the target.
