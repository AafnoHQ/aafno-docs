# Highest honorific — Ati Uchcha Aadar

**अति उच्च आदर (Ati Uchcha Aadar) · Draft research reference.** The forms, grammatical analyses, spellings, translations and usage claims on this page await linguistic and native-speaker validation. Keep them out of learner-facing instruction, answer keys and reference entries until reviewed.

## Sources and terminology

The project contributor supplied *Nepali Highest Honorific (Ati Uchcha Aadar) Conjugation Guide* as `Honorific guide.rtf` in the curriculum discussion. The research notes below identify its proposed forms and summarise its claims and example situations. The attachment is held separately. Authorship and redistribution rights have yet to be established.

The contributor separately reported these forms from family usage: **hoisinchha, gaisinchha, basisyo, khaisyo, gaisyo, aisyo**. These are working Romanisations as reported for this project. The family report attests usage in that setting. The guide separately proposes broader forms and rules. Shared or similar spellings across the two sources do not establish agreement, productivity or distribution. The reported association with Bahun and Chhetri families is neither universal within those communities nor exclusive to them.

**Highest honorific (Ati Uchcha Aadar)** replaces the provisional label *Family high honorific*. Record family use as contextual metadata with the relationship, setting and speech community where documented. Prior catalogue names, targets and IDs are retained. The approved G25–G31 rows also retain their exact wording.

## Formation and agreement claims to investigate

The guide proposes a full construction using the main verb's `-i` stem and inflected **बक्सिनु** (*baksinu*), as in *khaibaksincha*. It describes a spoken pattern with `-is-` and proposed third-person singular endings, as in *khaisincha*, and associates forms such as `-isinu` and `-isyo` with everyday Kathmandu speech. It treats जानु as irregular, with *ga-* throughout, and gives the pair *gaibaksinu / gaisinu*. The project discussion records the proposed infinitive spellings खाइबक्सिनु / खाइसिनु for खानु and गइबक्सिनु / गइसिनु for जानु. Validate those spellings against usage. It claims third-person singular endings in both direct respectful address (including *hajur* and *tapai*) and third-person reference. Check the applicability and exceptions of each generalisation, including agreement on auxiliaries and how person, number and clause type interact.

### Negatives and other verbs

| Guide label | Forms reported in the guide | Proposed change to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Negative imperative | नखाइस्यो (*Nakhaisyo*), नगइस्यो (*Nagaisyo*) | Prefix `na-` |
| Negative present | खाइसिँदैन (*Khaisidaina*) | Replace `-incha` with `-idaina` |
| Negative past | खाइसिएन (*Khaisiena*) | Replace `-iyo` with `-iena` |
| Negative future | खाइसिने छैन (*Khaisine chaina*) | Replace `-inecha` with `-ine chaina` |

The guide gives *aaisyo / aaisincha / aaisiyo* for आउनु, *basisyo / basisincha / basisiyo* for बस्नु, and *garisyo / garisincha / garisiyo* for गर्नु. These sequences still require validation. Preserve the source distinction between suggested request forms such as *khaisyo* and suggested past forms such as *khaisiyo*.

## Validation and curriculum requirements

- Investigate every applicable verb and construction across tense and aspect, polarity, questions, imperatives and requests, modality, compounds, complex sentences including conditionals and reported speech, and evidentiality, inference and discovery. Record applicability, attested variants and evidence gaps per construction. Leave unknown forms unspecified.
- Check Devanagari spelling, Romanisation, pronunciation, formation and morphological productivity. Test irregular stems, including the proposed *ga-* rule for जानु, and the claimed third-person singular pattern in direct address and third-person reference. Check polarity, auxiliaries and case marking. Verify Nepali tense, aspect and mood categories and the approximate English labels.
- Review the full and contracted forms with speakers who naturally use the register. Establish their relationship, distribution and naturalness across actual spoken and written settings, contexts and communities. Record how speakers relate to one another, intended meaning, available contrasts and acceptable alternatives. Collect and review native audio in context.
- In the future [Verb Conjugation Reference](../product/language-reference.md#verb-conjugation-reference), provide a selectable highest-honorific register with reviewed full and contracted variants distinguished where supported. Use contextually valid variants in assessment, linked to the same reviewed examples and native audio. Carry evidence, provenance, rights and status through [Content QA](content-qa.md).
- Plan recognition, guided use and independent application through the [concept framework](grammar-concept-framework.md#highest-honorific-across-conjugations) and [teaching depth guide](grammar-teaching-depth.md). Resolve catalogue overlaps before detailed specifications.
