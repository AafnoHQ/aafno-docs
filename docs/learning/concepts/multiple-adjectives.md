## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Identify each property in a combined phrase.
- LO2: Distinguish juxtaposed and coordinated descriptions.
- LO3: Compare orders in context.
- LO4: Produce a reviewed combination.
- LO5: Flag naturalness judgments requiring evidence.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A noun phrase can identify an entity by more than one property. Phrases can juxtapose descriptors or connect them. Both the intended grouping and the situation matter when comparing alternatives.

## 4. Formation rules

Choose a house size and colour. Inspect the combined phrase and identify what each word describes. Then compare a room display where size and cleanliness are presented as two explicitly coordinated properties. Try an alternate order as a candidate under review.

**Worked models.** ठूलो रातो घर / Thulo raato ghar / A big red house (Use size and colour as joint identification cues.) Compare राम्रो नयाँ किताब / Raamro nayaã kitaab / A nice new book (Evaluation and age in a book recommendation.) The examples are instructional candidates.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Do not import an English sequence of adjective classes. Orders may vary by emphasis, conventional combinations, semantic scope and prosody. Agreement and connector rules are separate targets.

### Compare alternative orders

Display the two house descriptions in a size-focused scene and a colour-focused scene. Change only order, not selected attributes. Record judgments about naturalness and which property sounds prominent independently. If the same interpretation is recoverable in both orders, that alone does not establish the same pragmatic effect.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

A learner can sound plausible while using a cue unrelated to the target. Test what they inferred with the two independent prompts in section 12.

**Corrective cue for the first prompt.** Underline the connection and compare with the two descriptors next to each other in row 1.

**Corrective cue for the second prompt.** If one property is missing, check the listener’s two selection clues and retry. Log plausible unreviewed orders for review.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) if context permits more than one answer or the variant has not been reviewed.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scenario.** A renter weighs size and cleanliness while a house seeker uses size and colour as search clues. Compare the two descriptions in their own settings before ranking alternative orders. Use the utterances from section 6 in the specified turns. Validate their naturalness in the exchange before using them with learners.

## 12. Activity types

References to rows are to section 6. Provide Devanagari, Romanisation and English by default. For unaided production, hide examples and target expressions until after the response. A scaffolded response is recorded as guided practice.

### First evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** Which phrase explicitly links its two descriptors with a connector?
- **Expected response.** Row 3 uses र / Ra / and.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Score this response independently of the other activities.
- **Feedback and retry.** Underline the connection and compare with the two descriptors next to each other in row 1.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.

## 13. Mastery evidence

In a fresh scenario, ask which properties are conveyed, whether they are explicitly joined, and what order is reviewed for that context. Test independent phrase production separately. Record what learners actually identify, interpret, select or produce against the applicable local outcome. Keep uncertainty visible and avoid inferring one outcome from evidence for another.
