## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise familiar lexical gender contrasts.
- LO2: Separate lexical information from agreement.
- LO3: Identify when gender is unspecified.
- LO4: Evaluate ending-based hypotheses.
- LO5: Interpret a new role or relationship without unsupported inference.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Some nouns encode natural gender lexically. Grammatical classifications can affect agreement, but do not independently establish an individual's gender. A role noun can leave gender unspecified in context.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare relationship pairs first and state what each lexical choice conveys. Then ask what the teacher sentence establishes in a scene without identity information. In the girl example distinguish the lexical clue from the candidate predicate ending.

**Worked models.** केटा / केटी / Keta / Keti / Boy / girl Compare छोरा / छोरी / Chhora / Chhori / Son / daughter Use the situations in section 6 to establish the intended readings.

## 5. Variations and agreement

A recurring vowel across lexical pairs is not a universal ending rule or proof of productive derivation. Compare an inanimate noun with a similar ending. Check compatible adjective and verb patterns separately, including honorific contexts.

### Limits of inference

Contrast a lexical relation that supplies natural-gender information with a predictable grammatical agreement pattern only after learners can explain each type of evidence separately. Compare the teacher and girl contexts without treating either ending as a complete paradigm. Ask speakers whether the same respectful predicate can occur with different referents and whether potentially gendered role labels are neutral in the specified context.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Likely misunderstanding.** A learner may infer natural gender from a vowel or an honorific ending without knowing the referent.

**Retry.** Ask whether each item names a relation or a role, then retry with a new role. For the contrast target, name the referent and give a clue beyond the vowel. Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft usage scenario.** In an arrival scene use row 4. Supply no information about identity, and ask what remains unknown. Compare a separate kinship exchange where a family term itself conveys the relationship. Check the turns with speakers in the intended context before using them as learner-facing models; no frequency or distribution is inferred.

## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** an understood role or kinship relation.
- **Helpful.** familiar family vocabulary.
- **Co-taught.** contrasting role nouns and lexical pairs.
- **Extension.** honorific agreement and derivational patterns.

For oral activities, supply enough context through audio or the scene so Devanagari literacy is not an assessment prerequisite.

## 12. Activity types

Row references below refer to the contextual examples in section 6. Use all three language representations by default; adjust visibility only for the evidence target.

### Initial target

- **Learner prompt.** Which family terms give gender information, and does the teacher scenario supply it?
- **Expected response.** The son/daughter lexemes do. The scenario does not establish the teacher's gender.
- **Assessment target.** LO1 and LO3. Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** Ask whether each item names a relation or a role, then retry with a new role.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

### Contrast target

- **Learner prompt.** Does the shared final vowel in the girl and hat examples establish natural gender?
- **Expected response.** No. The lexical referent supplies gender information in the girl example; a hat is inanimate.
- **Assessment target.** LO4. Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** Name the referent and give a clue beyond the vowel.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Use a lexical pair, a role, a similar-ending inanimate noun and a respectful predicate. Separate recognition, inference limits and agreement. Record what the answer demonstrates against the local outcomes and keep stem, ending, case, agreement and related semantic evidence separate. Guided performance can inform feedback; mastery requires an independent task and flexible evidence where relevant.
