## 3. Meaning and usage

Situation interpretation distinguishes a state that holds, an activity, an occurrence or a developing process. These categories describe what happens or holds in context. They do not map directly onto present, past or progressive labels.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare holding knowledge with running and water boiling. Then use a contextual recognition contrast for an expression that can be glossed familiarity or recognition. Ask what the speaker is describing before classifying the situation.

**Worked illustrations.** मलाई थाहा छ। / Malaai thaahaa chha. / I know. (Knowledge holds in the intended scene.) Compare म उसलाई चिन्छु। / Ma uslaai chinchhu. / I know or recognise them. (Compare relationship familiarity and recognising a face; review exact contextual readings.)

## 5. Variations and agreement

Do not assume that states must be permanent, actions intentional or dynamic readings necessarily progressive. Nepali compatibility and coercion need evidence. English aspect restrictions do not supply Nepali rules.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Diagnose the interpretation.** Ask what holds of the speaker rather than what they did. Retry with a familiarity example.

**Retry without leaking the target.** Compare what changes while the pot is heating with what the runner does. Retry with a different activity and process. Use [variant-aware assessment](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) when a plausible response depends on a reading or form still under review.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scene.** A cook describes water boiling while someone else answers a question about what they know. Compare the different situation types without relying on English tense labels. Use the matching contextual utterances in section 6 and review their naturalness in the exchange.

## 12. Activity types

Row references are to section 6. Provide Devanagari, Romanisation and English by default. For scored work, hide any model answer, full gloss, row label or annotation that discloses the target. Keep the Nepali forms that learners must interpret or recognise visible. Supply lexical meanings without revealing the target form relationship or grammatical interpretation.

### LO1 — Interpret an encountered state description

- **Learner prompt.** Read or hear मलाई थाहा छ। / Malaai thaahaa chha. in reply to a question about a fact. Give lexical glosses only. Does the reply describe a state that holds or an action the speaker performs?
- **Expected response.** It describes a state of knowing in the scenario.
- **Assessment target.** LO1 only. Assess contextual situation meaning. Detailed aspect stays with G09 and experiencer constructions with G24. Do not award a second mastery result for these features.
- **Feedback and retry.** Ask what holds of the speaker rather than what they did. Retry with a familiarity example.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm the example, analysis and accepted responses before use.

### LO2 — Interpret a dynamic action or process

- **Learner prompt.** Listen to the running and boiling examples. Which describes an activity and which an unfolding process?
- **Expected response.** Running is the activity; the boiling report describes an unfolding process.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Assess contextual situation meaning. Detailed aspect stays with G09 and experiencer constructions with G24. Do not award a second mastery result for these features.
- **Feedback and retry.** Compare what changes while the pot is heating with what the runner does. Retry with a different activity and process.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm the example, analysis and accepted responses before use.

### LO3 — Distinguish situation meaning from tense and aspect cues

- **Learner prompt.** Read the habitual running report and the ongoing boiling report. Is a progressive construction required for a dynamic reading?
- **Expected response.** No. The running report can express a dynamic activity in a routine without a progressive predicate.
- **Assessment target.** LO3 only. Assess contextual situation meaning. Detailed aspect stays with G09 and experiencer constructions with G24. Do not award a second mastery result for these features.
- **Feedback and retry.** Separate what happens from how it is presented in time. Try the arrival report as another dynamic example.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm the example, analysis and accepted responses before use.

### LO4 — Use context to discriminate potential readings

- **Learner prompt.** Read the familiarity example after a neighbour discussion, then after a question about a face in a photo. Which interpretation does each context support for review?
- **Expected response.** Familiarity in the first scene and a possible recognition reading in the second. The isolated form alone does not settle every reading.
- **Assessment target.** LO4 only. Assess contextual situation meaning. Detailed aspect stays with G09 and experiencer constructions with G24. Do not award a second mastery result for these features.
- **Feedback and retry.** Compare what the prior question asks in the two cases; retry after an explicit familiarity cue.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm the example, analysis and accepted responses before use.

### LO5 — Explain why a lexical classification needs contextual evidence

- **Learner prompt.** Revisit म उसलाई चिन्छु। / Ma uslaai chinchhu. in the neighbour and photo contexts. Identify what each lead-in supports, and explain why classifying every use from just one of those contexts would go beyond the evidence.
- **Expected response.** The neighbour context supports familiarity. The photo context invites a recognition reading, which remains provisional. More examples with controlled contexts are needed before classifying other uses.
- **Assessment target.** LO5 only. Assess contextual situation meaning. Detailed aspect stays with G09 and experiencer constructions with G24. Do not award a second mastery result for these features.
- **Feedback and retry.** Point to a different scenario that could change what is being reported, then explain what evidence you would need.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm the example, analysis and accepted responses before use.
