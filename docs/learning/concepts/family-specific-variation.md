## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise documented language forms used within particular families.
- LO2: Understand the meaning of validated family-specific expressions and constructions.
- LO3: Identify corresponding forms in the documented reference variety.
- LO4: Interpret unfamiliar family-specific alternatives using context and existing grammatical knowledge.
- LO5: Appropriately use validated family-specific forms in relevant household contexts.
- LO6: Recognise when a family convention may not be shared by other Nepali speakers.
- LO7: Adapt between familiar household usage and other appropriate forms outside the family.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Family-specific variation refers to language conventions documented within particular households or extended families.

A family-specific label does not imply that the form is unique to that family.

Investigate variation involving:

- Grammar.
- Honorifics.
- Vocabulary.
- Pronunciation.
- Spoken reductions.
- Household expressions.
- Conversational conventions.
- Language mixing.

Distinguish three questions:

1. What does the form mean?
2. Is it attested?
3. Is it appropriate in this context?

A legitimate household form may not be appropriate in every setting.

A form should not be rejected merely because it is absent from a textbook.

Do not infer caste, ethnicity, regional or community distribution from individual household evidence.

## 4. Formation rules

This concept does not have a single universal formation rule.

Document individual variants and link them to canonical grammatical explanations.

Illustrative comparison:

तपाईं सन्चै हुनुहुन्छ?

Tapaai sanchai hunnuhunchha?

Are you well?

हजुर सन्चै होइसिन्छ?

Hajur sanchai hoisinchha?

Are you well?

These illustrate variation between honorific registers.

Compare with variation within a register:

गइबक्सिनु — Gaibaksinu — To go.

गइसिनु — Gaisinu — To go.

Both belong to the same highest-honorific level in the documented family usage.

The product owner explicitly confirmed interchangeability within their family.

Do not infer universal interchangeability across every context.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

Address:

- Assuming textbook Nepali is the only correct Nepali.
- Assuming a household form is universally used.
- Assigning different respect levels to full and contracted forms.
- Mixing incompatible agreement patterns.
- Using legitimate forms in unsuitable contexts.
- Assuming every unfamiliar form is legitimate variation.
- Confusing pronunciation variation with grammatical error.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment).

**Feedback and retry.** Ask where and with whom the form was heard. Give a reviewed reference-variety comparison and ask the learner to choose a form for a new relationship. Flag unfamiliar responses for review and recognise validated alternatives when they fit the context.

## 9. Natural usage

Collect contextual examples involving:

- Grandparents.
- Parents.
- Siblings.
- Younger relatives.
- Unfamiliar elders.
- Family gatherings.
- Messaging relatives.

These are research contexts, not prescribed honorific rules.

Where speakers consent, record:

- Relationship between speakers.
- Situation.
- Actual spoken form.
- Intended meaning.
- Alternatives considered natural.
- Whether alternatives are interchangeable in that context.

Avoid publishing unnecessary personal information.

## 10. Prerequisites

Basic expressions are helpful.

Pronouns are required for relevant address patterns.

Honorific agreement is required for corresponding productive outcomes.

Vocabulary may be co-taught.

Basic grammatical constructions are required for structural comparison.

Register awareness may be co-taught.

Reference-variety knowledge is helpful.

Devanagari literacy is not required.

## 11. Teaching depth

| Teaching depth | Outcomes | Observable performance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | LO1, LO2, LO6 | Recognise variation and interpret reviewed familiar household examples. |
| Guided application | LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6 | Compare corresponding forms and choose a validated alternative with contextual cues. |
| Independent application | LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6, LO7 | Interpret unfamiliar reviewed recordings and respond in a suitable form. |
| Flexible application | LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6, LO7 | Adapt between household and other contexts as the relationship or audience changes. |

Heritage pathway:

Recognise existing household knowledge → understand reference forms → expand grammar → adapt to unfamiliar contexts.

Beginner pathway:

Acquire foundational reference forms → encounter relevant validated alternatives → develop receptive flexibility → select appropriate forms.

Do not require beginners to memorise every household variant.

## 12. Activity types

Include:

- Matching equivalent expressions.
- Listening to different speakers.
- Identifying contextual differences.
- Adapting expressions.
- Family conversation practice.
- Interpreting unfamiliar household expressions.

Introductory awareness questions may cover:

- Gaibaksinu and Gaisinu belonging to the same register.
- Limits of household attestation.
- Acceptance of validated alternatives.
- Appropriate interpretation of unfamiliar expressions.

However, theoretical awareness questions alone are insufficient for mastery.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Assess:

- Recognition.
- Comprehension.
- Correspondence between alternatives.
- Receptive transfer.
- Productive use.
- Contextual awareness.
- Adaptation.

Independent task:

The learner encounters two validated recordings of speakers using different household conventions, interprets their meaning and responds appropriately.

Learners do not need to reproduce every variant they hear.

Selecting a preference, reporting family usage or completing a reference lookup does not establish mastery.
