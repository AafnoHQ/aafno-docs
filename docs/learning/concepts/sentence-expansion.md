## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Identify the core proposition and needed participants.
- LO2: Add a suitable time or frequency expression.
- LO3: Add place or destination information with the intended meaning.
- LO4: Add manner or another suitable circumstance.
- LO5: Combine modifiers while preserving grammatical coherence and scope.
- LO6: Independently expand a familiar proposition and explain what changed.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Expansion adds information a listener needs while retaining the intended core proposition. Time, place, frequency and manner can answer different questions. A modifier may be optional in a frame where some participant or complement is needed.

## 4. Formation rules

Choose a base clause, ask what information the listener needs, then attach a suitable expression in a reviewed position. Recheck the predicate frame and the scope of each addition. Compare the base, destination, frequency and non-finite extension below. Keep adverb, postposition and clause formation with their canonical owners.

### Worked example

Start with ‘I go’. Add the destination to answer where; add today to locate an occasion; then compare a separate routine version with every day. Add a manner expression only if it makes sense for that event. Compare the after-work construction as a later clause-level extension.

**Draft model.** म घर जान्छु। / Ma ghar jaanchhu. / I go home. → म आज घर जान्छु। / Ma aaja ghar jaanchhu. / I will go home today. Compare म हरेक दिन घर जान्छु। / Ma harek din ghar jaanchhu. / I go home every day, and म बिस्तारै घर जान्छु। / Ma bistaarai ghar jaanchhu. / I go home slowly.

## 5. Variations and agreement

A place expression can be a location or destination depending on the predicate. Multiple modifiers can have competing orders or scopes. Review omission, agreement, case-marked circumstances and non-finite combinations before using them. Adding a dependent clause extends beyond phrase-only expansion.

### Context and contrast

A destination may be part of the predicate frame even where it can be omitted in context. Time and frequency describe different temporal readings; combine them only when their scope and situation are intelligible.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

If a modifier changes the intended frequency or crowds out a necessary participant, remove additions one at a time and recover the base proposition. Add back a single expression answering the cue and recheck scope before combining two.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for reviewed alternatives, contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

**Targeted retry.** If a learner replaces a destination with a manner word, ask which information the listener still needs. Retry by restoring the destination and adding manner without changing the original event.

## 9. Natural usage

Use a routine-planning exchange. Add one detail when the listener asks where, how often or how. Compare what listeners learned with each version. Use the proposed builder for scaffolded practice and follow with free production.

Draft exchange for review. The follow-up asks for frequency. The reply preserves the destination and adds frequency rather than changing the core motion event.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Required: a meaningful base clause.
- Helpful: time, place and manner vocabulary.
- Co-teach: modifier placement and attachment.
- Extension: non-finite and subordinate expansions under G20 and G22.

A familiar base proposition and relevant time, place and manner expressions. Co-teach modifier choice. Non-finite and subordinate extensions depend on reviewed clause patterns. Requirements vary by outcome and depth. Use contextual audio and glosses for oral tasks so learners can demonstrate the target without script literacy.

## 12. Activity types

- Add or remove time, place, manner or frequency in the proposed builder.
- Use a listener’s follow-up questions to add details.
- Produce a new expanded routine without tiles or a model.

### Draft activity prototype

- **Prompt.** Expand an ‘I go’ answer with destination, today, frequency in a separate version, and manner.
- **Expected response.** म घर जान्छु। / Ma ghar jaanchhu. / I go home. → म आज घर जान्छु। / Ma aaja ghar jaanchhu. / I will go home today. Compare म हरेक दिन घर जान्छु। / Ma harek din ghar jaanchhu. / I go home every day, and म बिस्तारै घर जान्छु। / Ma bistaarai ghar jaanchhu. / I go home slowly.
- **Feedback.** Identify the question each addition answers. If one version combines several modifiers, have the learner paraphrase what each modifies.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Give the learner a new familiar proposition and ask for two appropriate additions without a model. Ask them to paraphrase the final meaning and explain the scope. A guided slot-fill can record scaffolded evidence separately.
