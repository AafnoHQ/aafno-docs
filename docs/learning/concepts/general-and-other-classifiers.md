## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise a general counting form in a reviewed object phrase.
- LO2: Distinguish objects, measures and containers.
- LO3: Select a counting expression for a given unit.
- LO4: Identify limits of form-generation and omission rules.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A counting expression identifies what a number counts. An object classifier, a measurement unit and a container expression can contribute different unit meanings. They need not have identical grammar.

## 4. Formation rules

Start with books and apples as individual objects. In a food-order scene replace an object count with kilograms, litres or bottles depending on the intended unit. Identify exactly what the numeral now counts.

**Worked models.** तीनवटा किताब / Tinwata kitaab / Three books (Individual objects.) Compare दुईवटा स्याउ / Duiwata syaau / Two apples (General counting form joined to the numeral in this model.)

## 5. Variations and agreement

Compare candidates एउटा / Eutaa / one item and एकवटा / Ekwata / one item rather than generating all forms by concatenation. Review joining, alternation, classifier omission, and unit versus classifier analyses independently.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

The same numeral can count different units. Ask the learner to choose what the speaker is actually measuring.

**Example corrective retry.** Separate the numeral and the counting element, then find the same element in the book phrase. Use [variant-aware assessment](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and potentially valid but unreviewed alternatives.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scenario.** Compare buying apples, ordering bottled water and measuring water for a recipe. Ask what one unit means in each exchange. Use the appropriate contextual models from section 6 for the response. Review naturalness and pronunciation before using this exchange as learner-facing material.

## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** A known referent and an indication of whether the task needs a count, measure or container.
- **Helpful.** Familiarity with the scenario vocabulary.
- **Co-taught.** Use the contrast in the guided example. Supply values, number words, clocks, labels or totals whenever they are supporting information.
- **Extension.** Refer to related canonical targets for independent evidence. Provide sufficient audio or scene cues for learners working without Devanagari.

### LO1 — Recognise a general counting form in a reviewed object phrase

- **Learner prompt.** Identify the general counting form in the apple phrase.
- **Expected response.** वटा / Wata / general counting form.
- **Assessment target.** LO1 only. Score recognition or selection of the correct counting expression or unit. Do not infer numerical composition mastery or general countability from that choice.
- **Feedback and retry.** Separate the numeral and the counting element, then find the same element in the book phrase.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.

### LO2 — Distinguish objects, measures and containers

- **Learner prompt.** In the last two rows, what does two count in each?
- **Expected response.** Litres in one and bottles in the other.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Score recognition or selection of the correct counting expression or unit. Do not infer numerical composition mastery or general countability from that choice.
- **Feedback and retry.** Use a measuring jug and two differently sized bottles to show why the units differ, then retry with kilograms.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.

### LO3 — Select a counting expression for a given unit

- **Learner prompt.** For a recipe specifying volume, choose the appropriate unit word from supplied litre and bottle options. Do not construct a whole phrase.
- **Expected response.** लिटर / Litar / litre.
- **Assessment target.** LO3 only. Score recognition or selection of the correct counting expression or unit. Do not infer numerical composition mastery or general countability from that choice.
- **Feedback and retry.** Return to what the recipe measures and choose again for a separate container-based order.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.

### LO4 — Identify limits of form-generation and omission rules

- **Learner prompt.** Compare the Nepali object and measure phrases in rows 2–4 with only the numeral and noun glosses supplied. Identify the forms used with each. Do these examples alone justify using one counting form with every noun or spelling it the same way?
- **Expected response.** The apple phrase uses वटा / Wata, the rice phrase किलो / Kilo, and the water phrase लिटर / Litar. The forms cannot be generalised to every noun or spelling context from these examples.
- **Assessment target.** LO4 only. Score recognition or selection of the correct counting expression or unit. Do not infer numerical composition mastery or general countability from that choice.
- **Feedback and retry.** Compare with a measured substance and a one-object candidate before making a narrower claim.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.

### LO3b — Select a general counting form for objects

- **Learner prompt.** A stocktake shows three separate books. With the complete phrase hidden, supply तीन / Tin / three and किताब / Kitaab / book as separate cue cards. Give only the counting form appropriate for the books.
- **Expected response.** वटा / Wata / general counting form. Accept the classifier identified as the counting element in a supported variant. Do not require an assembled phrase.
- **Assessment target.** LO3, individual-object classifier selection. Score separately from measurement-unit selection. G06-009 owns phrase composition.
- **Feedback and retry.** If you name a unit of weight or volume, point to a single book and ask what is being counted. Retry with two apples and their numeral and noun supplied.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm contextual suitability and acceptable forms, including any alternatives or possible omission, before use.
