## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise collection nouns.
- LO2: Distinguish one group from multiple groups.
- LO3: Distinguish groups from their members.
- LO4: Interpret group versus member focus.
- LO5: Explain why semantic plurality does not determine agreement.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A collective noun presents a group as a unit, even when the unit contains many members. Counting groups and counting individual members can yield different answers.

## 4. Formation rules

Use the sports examples. Mark a box for each team, then separately count players. Keep the noun ‘team’ when changing the number of boxes. Switch to ‘players’ when counting individuals.

**Worked models.** एउटा टोली आयो। / Eutaa toli aayo. / One team arrived. Compare दुईवटा टोली आए। / Duiwata toli aae. / Two teams arrived. Use the situations in section 6 to establish the intended readings.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Review group versus member construal and agreement in context. Morphological number and numeral constructions belong to their separate owners.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Likely misunderstanding.** Counting people instead of group units gives an unsupported roster size.

**Retry.** Count boxes before people and retry with teams of different sizes. For the contrast target, replace the team boxes with individual players and identify what the numeral now counts. Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft usage scenario.** One spectator says row 1, while another checks the roster. Compare row 3 as an answer to a question about players, then use row 2 to report a second group. Check the turns with speakers in the intended context before using them as learner-facing models; no frequency or distribution is inferred.

## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** ability to distinguish groups and their members in a scenario.
- **Helpful.** basic number vocabulary.
- **Co-taught.** group and member diagrams.
- **Extension.** agreement and classifier forms.

For oral activities, supply enough context through audio or the scene so Devanagari literacy is not an assessment prerequisite.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Use one family, several teams and a member-level follow-up. Ask what is counted and whether number or agreement is separately observable. Record what the answer demonstrates against the local outcomes and keep stem, ending, case, agreement and related semantic evidence separate. Guided performance can inform feedback; mastery requires an independent task and flexible evidence where relevant.
