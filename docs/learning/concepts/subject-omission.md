# Subject omission

**Phase 2A.2 Step 3A specification draft.** Product-owner approval covers the pilot direction. Language examples and assessment alternatives await review. [Pilot index](index.md) · [Content QA](../content-qa.md).

## 1. Concept identity

- Catalogue ID: **G01-004** · [G01 — Sentence structure](../grammar-catalogue/grammar-foundations.md#g01).
- English title: Subject omission.
- Proposed Nepali terminology: कर्ता लोप.
- Romanisation: Kartaa lop.
- Concept type: Syntactic and contextual construction.
- Catalogue learning outcome: Understand and construct sentences with an omitted subject and recover the subject from context where appropriate.
- Specification status: Draft implementing the approved pilot direction.
- Validation status: Linguistic and native-speaker review pending. Publication eligibility unestablished.
- CEFR assignment: Unassigned.

## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise sentences with an omitted subject.
- LO2: Recover the understood subject from context.
- LO3: Interpret relevant agreement clues.
- LO4: Produce appropriate subject omission where the referent is recoverable.
- LO5: Recognise when retaining or restoring an explicit subject improves clarity.
- LO6: Preserve appropriate agreement when omitting the subject.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Nepali can permit subjects to remain unexpressed when they are recoverable through context and grammatical clues.

Example:

म घर जान्छु।

Ma ghar jaanchhu.

I go home / I will go home.

घर जान्छु।

Ghar jaanchhu.

I go home / I will go home.

Both may express the same intended subject in an appropriate context.

Do not imply that explicit subjects are inherently unnatural.

Do not imply that omission is always possible.

Do not imply that an omitted subject always means "I".

The symbol ∅ may be used in grammatical annotations but should not appear as ordinary learner-facing sentence text.

## 4. Formation rules

The basic instructional pattern is:

Explicit subject + remaining sentence → remaining sentence with recoverable understood subject.

Examples:

म चिया पिउँछु।

Ma chiya piunchhu.

I drink tea.

चिया पिउँछु।

Chiya piunchhu.

I drink tea.

तिमी फुटबल खेल्छौ?

Timi football khelchhau?

Do you play football?

फुटबल खेल्छौ?

Football khelchhau?

Do you play football?

हामी भोलि जान्छौँ।

Haami bholi jaanchhaũ.

We will go tomorrow.

भोलि जान्छौँ।

Bholi jaanchhaũ.

We will go tomorrow.

Preserve appropriate agreement.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Use the [present habitual agreement examples](present-habitual.md#present-habitual-agreement) and the corresponding [G08 rules](../grammar-catalogue/verbs-and-conjugation.md#g08).

Example:

उहाँ काम गर्नुहुन्छ।

Uhã kaam garnuhunchha.

He or she works, respectfully.

काम गर्नुहुन्छ।

Kaam garnuhunchha.

Works / you work, respectfully, depending on context.

Respectful agreement does not necessarily uniquely identify the referent.

Investigate highest-honorific omission and agreement without inventing forms.

## 7. Related constructions and contrasts

Distinguish:

- G01-004 basic subject omission.
- G01-002 sentence components.
- G08 agreement.
- G24-005 passive agent omission.
- G29-007 advanced argument omission.
- G29-008 clause-level ellipsis.
- G29-009 conversational fragments.

Contrast:

चिया पिउँछु।

Chiya piunchhu.

I drink tea.

पिएँ।

Piẽ.

I drank it.

चिया।

Chiya.

Tea.

These are not necessarily instances of the same grammatical omission process.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

Address:

- Assuming subjects must always be explicit.
- Assuming an omitted subject always means "I".
- Changing agreement when omitting the subject.
- Omitting a subject when the referent becomes unclear.
- Treating explicit subjects as errors.
- Assuming respectful agreement uniquely identifies a referent.

Use contrasts such as:

घर जान्छौ।

Ghar jaanchhau.

You go home.

घर जान्छु।

Ghar jaanchhu.

I go home.

**Feedback and retry.** Ask who is speaking or being addressed and what the verb ending and previous turn tell them. Retry with an unambiguous context. If two people remain plausible, restore the subject. Accept an explicit subject as grammatical where appropriate and track omission evidence separately.

## 9. Natural usage

आज के गर्छौ?

Aaja ke garchhau?

What are you doing today?

काम गर्छु।

Kaam garchhu.

I will work.

कहिले घर जान्छौ?

Kahile ghar jaanchhau?

When are you going home?

छ बजे घर जान्छु।

Chha baje ghar jaanchhu.

I will go home at six.

भोलि आउँछौ?

Bholi aauchhau?

Will you come tomorrow?

भोलि आउँछु।

Bholi aauchhu.

I will come tomorrow.

Contrast with explicit subject use:

म चिया पिउँछु, तर ऊ कफी पिउँछ।

Ma chiya piunchhu, tara u coffee piunchha.

I drink tea, but he drinks coffee.

Explicit subjects may clarify contrast.

## 10. Prerequisites

Sentence components are required.

Pronouns are helpful.

Agreement may be co-taught initially and becomes important for independent application.

Advanced discourse theory is not required for introductory learning.

Devanagari literacy is not required.

## 11. Teaching depth

| Teaching depth | Outcomes | Observable performance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | LO1, LO2, LO3 | Identify the understood subject using a preceding turn and an agreement clue. |
| Guided application | LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6 | Omit or restore a subject from a supplied exchange while preserving agreement. |
| Independent application | LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6 | Discuss plans and recover the referent without a supplied frame. |
| Flexible application | LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6 | Adapt omission and explicit reference when speakers or topics shift. |

## 12. Activity types

Include:

- Recovering the understood subject.
- Removing a recoverable explicit subject.
- Identifying ambiguity.
- Selecting appropriate agreement.
- Listening to contextual dialogues.
- Restoring an explicit subject where necessary.
- Spoken roleplay.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Assess:

- Recognition.
- Referent recovery.
- Agreement interpretation.
- Appropriate production.
- Clarity.
- Meaning preservation.
- Transfer.

An independent task could involve discussing weekend plans.

A grammatically correct explicit subject does not automatically demonstrate subject omission mastery.
