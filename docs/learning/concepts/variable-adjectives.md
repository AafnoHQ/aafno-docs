## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise alternation across matched forms.
- LO2: Group reviewed forms by their lexical base.
- LO3: Distinguish alternation from a different adjective.
- LO4: Explain why an ending alone does not identify a class.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A variable adjective has more than one form in a reviewed constructional pattern. The class decision concerns the behaviour of the same lexeme, whereas selecting an agreement form is G04-004.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare the row of three forms for one descriptor. Hold its basic meaning constant while comparing masculine, feminine and plural example environments. Check a second lexeme, then contrast an unchanged form in the same environments.

**Worked models.** राम्रो / राम्री / राम्रा / Raamro / Raamri / Raamraa / Good or nice, alternating forms (Compare the reviewed noun environments before interpreting a particular form.) Compare ठूलो / ठूली / ठूला / Thulo / Thuli / Thulaa / Big, alternating forms (Check whether the expected variants fit the referent and frame.) The examples are instructional candidates.

## 5. Variations and agreement

These illustrative series are evidence of candidate classes, not an exhaustive agreement table. Test noun, agreement and register contrasts independently before generalising an ending rule.

Compare राम्रो / Raamro / good with राम्री / Raamri / good by locating the recurring lexical material and the changing end. Ask reviewers how to segment the forms and represent vowel or spelling alternations. English meaning cannot predict the morphology.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

A learner can sound plausible while using a cue unrelated to the target. Test what they inferred with the two independent prompts in section 12.

**Corrective cue for the first prompt.** Hold the quality fixed and compare the shapes; do not choose an ending for a new noun yet.

**Corrective cue for the second prompt.** Ask which forms have actually been observed, then request another context.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) if context permits more than one answer or the variant has not been reviewed.

## 12. Activity types

References to rows are to section 6. Provide Devanagari, Romanisation and English by default. For unaided production, hide examples and target expressions until after the response. A scaffolded response is recorded as guided practice.

### First evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** Group the three forms in row 1 by lexical base. Are they forms of one descriptor or three different descriptors?
- **Expected response.** They are three alternating forms of one descriptor meaning good or nice in the intended contexts.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Score this response independently of the other activities.
- **Feedback and retry.** Hold the quality fixed and compare the shapes; do not choose an ending for a new noun yet.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.

### Second evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** An unfamiliar descriptor ends like one form in row 1 but no other uses are given. Can its class be established?
- **Expected response.** No. Compare its forms in more than one suitable context.
- **Assessment target.** LO4 only. Score this response independently of the other activities.
- **Feedback and retry.** Ask which forms have actually been observed, then request another context.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Sort unfamiliar sets into supported alternation, lexical substitution and insufficient evidence. Explain the comparison that supports the classification without choosing a new agreement form. Record what learners actually identify, interpret, select or produce against the applicable local outcome. Keep uncertainty visible and avoid inferring one outcome from evidence for another.
