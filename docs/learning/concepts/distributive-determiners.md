## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Identify the domain of a distributive determiner.
- LO2: Contrast individual distribution and collective reference.
- LO3: Distinguish determiner distribution from repeated numerical marking.
- LO4: Produce a reviewed distributive phrase.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A distributive determiner can apply a rule or event separately to members or occasions. A totality expression may describe everyone acting together or separately depending on the predicate and scenario.

## 4. Formation rules

Draw the relevant students, then link one application of a rule to each student. Contrast a teacher addressing everyone together with individually checking each student. In a routine, distribute the same expectation over days.

**Worked models.** प्रत्येक विद्यार्थी / Pratyek vidyaarthi / Each student (A rule applies individually to members of the class.) Compare हरेक दिन / Harek din / Every day (The recurring domain is days.)

## 5. Variations and agreement

Compare प्रत्येक / Pratyek / each and हरेक / Harek / every in specific frames; their register and distribution need review. सबै / Sabai / all does not force collective meaning. Keep noun number and predicate agreement outside this target.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

Cover the numerical foil and identify who or what receives separate application before discussing quantities.

**Example corrective retry.** Mark the days rather than the people affected, then retry with a new weekly schedule. Use [variant-aware assessment](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and potentially valid but unreviewed alternatives.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scenario.** Contrast a rule applied separately to each student with an announcement addressed to the whole class. Compare a recurring expectation over days. Use the appropriate contextual models from section 6 for the response. Review naturalness and pronunciation before using this exchange as learner-facing material.

## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** A defined set and whether the application occurs per member or occasion.
- **Helpful.** Familiarity with the scenario vocabulary.
- **Co-taught.** Use the contrast in the guided example. Supply values, number words, clocks, labels or totals whenever they are supporting information.
- **Extension.** Refer to related canonical targets for independent evidence. Provide sufficient audio or scene cues for learners working without Devanagari.

### LO1 — Identify the domain of a distributive determiner

- **Learner prompt.** What is the domain in the every-day example?
- **Expected response.** The days of the stated routine.
- **Assessment target.** LO1 only. Score domain and individual application. Do not award repeated-number evidence or infer that noun plural or predicate agreement has been demonstrated.
- **Feedback and retry.** Mark the days rather than the people affected, then retry with a new weekly schedule.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.

### LO2 — Contrast individual distribution and collective reference

- **Learner prompt.** In one scene the teacher addresses the whole class together. In another, one rule is checked for each student. Which scene explicitly requires individual application?
- **Expected response.** The second scene. A phrase meaning all students does not settle the choice without context.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Score domain and individual application. Do not award repeated-number evidence or infer that noun plural or predicate agreement has been demonstrated.
- **Feedback and retry.** Draw a group arrow for the first and individual arrows for the second. Retry with a new example.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.

### LO3 — Distinguish determiner distribution from repeated numerical marking

- **Learner prompt.** Which sample specifies an amount per recipient as well as a distribution pattern?
- **Expected response.** The repeated-number book phrase; it specifies two per recipient in the scene.
- **Assessment target.** LO3 only. Score domain and individual application. Do not award repeated-number evidence or infer that noun plural or predicate agreement has been demonstrated.
- **Feedback and retry.** Separate which members get counted from how much each gets and repeat with different quantities supplied.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.

### LO4 — Produce a reviewed distributive phrase

- **Learner prompt.** Give a Nepali phrase for ‘every day’ with the word for day supplied but the model hidden.
- **Expected response.** हरेक दिन / Harek din / every day.
- **Assessment target.** LO4 only. Score domain and individual application. Do not award repeated-number evidence or infer that noun plural or predicate agreement has been demonstrated.
- **Feedback and retry.** If you named a single day, revisit the recurring schedule and apply the same requirement to each day.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.
