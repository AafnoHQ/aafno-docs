## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise a quality assertion.
- LO2: Identify the quality being predicated.
- LO3: Contrast predicative and attributive use.
- LO4: Produce a reviewed quality assertion.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A predicative adjective presents a quality as part of what the clause says about a participant. Attaching a quality inside a noun phrase can identify an item without making the same assertion.

## 4. Formation rules

Hold the house constant. Use a noun phrase as a listing label. In a reply about that house, put the quality within a predicate using the reviewed copular frame in row 2. Compare a second known object.

**Worked models.** राम्रो घर / Raamro ghar / A nice house (Property label.) Compare घर राम्रो छ। / Ghar raamro chha. / The house is nice. (Assert the quality of a house already identified.) The examples are instructional candidates.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Copular forms, time and adjective agreement can vary with context. Use reviewed predicate frames; detailed copula and agreement formation belong elsewhere. An answer fragment can communicate in dialogue without independently establishing productive clause evidence.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

A learner can sound plausible while using a cue unrelated to the target. Test what they inferred with the two independent prompts in section 12.

**Corrective cue for the first prompt.** Ask whether the speaker is naming an option or telling the listener what it is like.

**Corrective cue for the second prompt.** If you gave a room label, name the known room and assert the quality in a reviewed predicate construction.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) if context permits more than one answer or the variant has not been reviewed.

## 12. Activity types

References to rows are to section 6. Provide Devanagari, Romanisation and English by default. For unaided production, hide examples and target expressions until after the response. A scaffolded response is recorded as guided practice.

### First evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** Is row 1 a label or an assertion, and what about row 2?
- **Expected response.** Row 1 is a phrase label. Row 2 asserts that the house is nice.
- **Assessment target.** LO3 only. Score this response independently of the other activities.
- **Feedback and retry.** Ask whether the speaker is naming an option or telling the listener what it is like.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.

### Second evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** Tell a listener in a full Nepali sentence that the inspected room is large. Hide the models.
- **Expected response.** कोठा ठूलो छ। / Kothaa thulo chha. / The room is large.
- **Assessment target.** LO4 only. Score this response independently of the other activities.
- **Feedback and retry.** If you gave a room label, name the known room and assert the quality in a reviewed predicate construction.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Use unseen quality assertions and noun phrase foils, then elicit a full predicate after a new observation. Score the quality assertion separately from choosing a copular variant. Record what learners actually identify, interpret, select or produce against the applicable local outcome. Keep uncertainty visible and avoid inferring one outcome from evidence for another.
