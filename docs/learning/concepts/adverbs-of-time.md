## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Identify the time cue.
- LO2: Interpret the event relative to a reference point.
- LO3: Distinguish time from duration, frequency or verb tense.
- LO4: Produce an appropriate time expression.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A temporal adverb indicates when something occurs relative to a day, moment or reference event. Its contribution differs from duration, repetition and tense carried by the predicate.

## 4. Formation rules

Set the conversation day on a calendar. Place yesterday, today and tomorrow with respect to it. Then use now, earlier and later with a reference moment in context; a time cue and the predicate jointly support the reading.

**Worked models.** हिजो / Hijo / Yesterday (One day before the conversation day.) Compare आज / Aaja / Today (Conversation day.) The examples are instructional candidates.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Later and earlier can depend on a reference event. More than one position can be possible. Verb tense and aspect remain G09 and placement effects remain G05-010.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

A learner can sound plausible while using a cue unrelated to the target. Test what they inferred with the two independent prompts in section 12.

**Corrective cue for the first prompt.** Move the conversation marker first, then find the next day rather than counting the trip length.

**Corrective cue for the second prompt.** Mark the current day and retry with a time word, leaving verb tense outside the task.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) if context permits more than one answer or the variant has not been reviewed.
