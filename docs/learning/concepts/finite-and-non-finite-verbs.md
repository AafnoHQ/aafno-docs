## 3. Meaning and usage

Finiteness concerns inflection and grammatical organisation, while dependency concerns how an expression works within a larger construction. A subordinate clause can contain an inflected verb, and a conversational fragment may sound complete with context. No one surface cue classifies all examples.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare the eating-then-going example: the eating expression depends on a larger clause, while the going form carries the main finite inflection. Then compare the lexical and auxiliary elements of the progressive. The larger predicate may be finite even if the lexical element itself is dependent.

**Worked illustrations.** खाना खाएर घर जान्छु। / Khaanaa khaaera ghar jaanchhu. / After eating I go home. (Dependent eating expression and main finite clause in this example.) Compare घर जान्छु। / Ghar jaanchhu. / I go home. (Inflected finite example.)

## 5. Variations and agreement

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Diagnose the interpretation.** Locate the form that expresses the main going event, then compare the standalone go reply.

**Retry without leaking the target.** Find the earlier event as presented in relation to the main clause. Try a reviewed parallel example. Use [variant-aware assessment](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) when a plausible response depends on a reading or form still under review.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scene.** A speaker describes a route home after a meal, then replies with a short movement report. Discuss why a shortened response can communicate successfully without classifying every fragment as finite. Use the matching contextual utterances in section 6 and review their naturalness in the exchange.
