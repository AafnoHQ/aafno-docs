## 3. Meaning and usage

As a working distinction, ask whether the listener can identify the referent and whether the speaker has a particular one in mind. A particular choice may be private; an open request may allow any suitable item.

## 4. Formation rules

Use the identical bare request for any spare book and after a shared discussion identifies one textbook. Then compare first mention of a purchase with the subsequent reference. Finally hold the search sentence constant while varying whether the speaker has already chosen a title.

**Worked models.** किताब चाहिन्छ। / Kitaab chaahinchha. / A book is needed, or I need a book. Compare किताब चाहिन्छ। / Kitaab chaahinchha. / I need the book. Use the situations in section 6 to establish the intended readings.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Nepali has no directly equivalent obligatory English article system. Bare nouns, demonstratives, numerals and indefinites are contextual cues, not one-to-one articles. Case and order may correlate with readings; test those patterns before teaching categorical rules.

### Track listener knowledge separately

After the purchase report, use the follow-up where both speakers can now identify the book. Before the first mention, the buyer can already have a particular book in mind, without the listener knowing it. In the open request, any suitable item can satisfy the need. Do not force every scene into a two-by-two table when the exchange does not establish the intended reading. Ask for a distinguishing description or whether any suitable item will do.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Likely misunderstanding.** A learner may assume the listener knows the intended item simply because the speaker has chosen one.

**Retry.** Replay the previous turn and identify what both speakers know. For the contrast target, ask separately whether one has been selected and whether the listener can tell which. Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 12. Activity types

Row references below refer to the contextual examples in section 6. Use all three language representations by default; adjust visibility only for the evidence target.

### Initial target

- **Learner prompt.** In which of the first two scenes can the listener already identify the intended book?
- **Expected response.** Scene 2 because the textbook was agreed. The phrasing alone does not settle identifiability.
- **Assessment target.** LO1 (identifiability). Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** Replay the previous turn and identify what both speakers know.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

### Contrast target

- **Learner prompt.** In row 6, the speaker has privately chosen a title in one scene and accepts any workbook in the other. Which is particular?
- **Expected response.** The private choice is particular even though the listener may not know it.
- **Assessment target.** LO4 (speaker particularity). Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** Ask separately whether one has been selected and whether the listener can tell which.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

### Track a referent across turns

- **Learner prompt.** In rows 4 and 5, when does the book first become part of the conversation, and is the second turn about the same book?
- **Expected response.** The purchase report introduces it. The follow-up picks up that same book in the supplied context.
- **Assessment target.** LO2, tracking first and subsequent mention. Score this separately from identifiability and speaker particularity.
- **Feedback and retry.** If you introduced a second book, check whether another book has been mentioned. Draw a link from the purchase report to the follow-up and try again.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Check whether the follow-up is natural and the reference is recoverable without additional cues.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Use fresh dialogues crossing shared identifiability and private particular intent; include first and later mention and a genuinely underspecified request. Score both judgments separately. Record what the answer demonstrates against the local outcomes and keep stem, ending, case, agreement and related semantic evidence separate. Guided performance can inform feedback; mastery requires an independent task and flexible evidence where relevant.
