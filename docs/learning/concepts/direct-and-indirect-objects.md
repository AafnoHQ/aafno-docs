# Direct and indirect objects

**Phase 2A.2 Step 3B Batch 1 specification draft.** [Batch register](../specification-batches.md#step-3b-batch-1) · [Content QA](../content-qa.md).

- CEFR assignment: Unassigned.

## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise a direct object relationship in a familiar predicate frame.
- LO2: Identify the recipient or beneficiary when the construction supports that role.
- LO3: Distinguish a transferred entity from its recipient.
- LO4: Interpret the same marked form in different predicate contexts.
- LO5: Use case as a supporting clue alongside predicate meaning.
- LO6: Explain object and recipient relationships in unfamiliar validated frames.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A transfer provides a clear contrast between what is transferred and who receives it. Other verbs may act on a person without a transfer. Use beneficiary and indirect-object labels only where the reviewed analysis supports them.

## 4. Formation rules

Begin with the predicate and its possible participant roles. For ‘give’, identify giver, item and recipient. Ask which expression fills each role. For a perception predicate, identify who is seen. Compare the hunger construction where the marked participant feels the state. Case morphology is taught by G14, and predicate frames by G07 and G24.

## 5. Variations and agreement

लाई / Laai can occur with recipients, some direct objects and experiencers. Review differential marking, order and agreement by predicate, and distinguish beneficiaries from recipients when the event does not entail transfer. Do not generalise from one marked form or one word order.

| मलाई भोक लाग्यो। | Malaai bhok laagyo. | I got hungry. | Experience: the speaker feels hunger; defer experiencer syntax to G24-012. |

## 7. Related constructions and contrasts

The same transfer example can assess sentence components, marking or object relationships. In this specification, the key distinction is the entity versus the recipient or beneficiary.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

If a learner treats the first लाई form as a recipient everywhere, ask whether anything changes hands. Retry with the same pronoun in a transfer and a perception or experience. Use the verb and event to decide which response description fits.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for reviewed alternatives, contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

Set up a conversation about who received what. Follow it with a comparison about seeing the same person. Ask learners to explain the roles before offering any grammatical terminology.

Draft exchange for review. The question singles out the recipient. In a separate retelling ask what was transferred. The two questions target two distinct roles.

| Turn | Devanagari | Romanisation | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Question | रामले कसलाई किताब दियो? | Raamle kaslaai kitaab diyo? | Who did Ram give a book to? |
| Reply | रामले सीतालाई किताब दियो। | Raamle Sitaalaai kitaab diyo. | Ram gave Sita a book. |

## 10. Prerequisites

Familiar transfer and perception vocabulary and an understood event. Co-teach basic semantic roles and case clues. Extend to more ambiguous beneficiary frames after review. Requirements vary by outcome and depth. Use contextual audio and glosses for oral tasks so learners can demonstrate the target without script literacy.

## 12. Activity types

- Match givers, entities and recipients to an event.
- Compare marked forms across transfer and perception.
- Ask a learner to explain who received what and transfer to a new frame.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Use two unfamiliar reviewed predicate frames including a person as the direct object in one. Ask for the affected or transferred entity and any recipient or beneficiary, and require a meaning-based explanation even when the same marking appears.
