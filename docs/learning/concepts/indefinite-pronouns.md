## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Distinguish unspecified people and things or events.
- LO2: Interpret affirmative and interrogative indefinite reference.
- LO3: Identify the contribution of negation.
- LO4: Contrast standalone reference with quantity modification.
- LO5: Choose a suitable indefinite in context.

## 3. Meaning and usage

An indefinite introduces an unidentified person or thing. Polarity changes the sentence interpretation, so a negative English gloss need not be the meaning of the pronoun.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare an arrival report, a question about an arrival and a denial. Keep the indefinite fixed and follow the predicate or question context. Switch to an event whose nature is unknown.

**Worked models.** कोही आयो। / Kohi aayo. / Someone came. Compare कोही आयो? / Kohi aayo? / Did anyone come? Use the situations in section 6 to establish the intended readings.

## 5. Variations and agreement

A following noun can give a quantity or subset reading. An unidentified person can still be someone particular to the speaker. Keep quantity, negation and specificity distinct.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Likely misunderstanding.** Treating the indefinite itself as ‘nobody’ gives the wrong interpretation in a question or affirmative report.

**Retry.** Keep the indefinite fixed and replay the two predicates. For the contrast target, ask whether the unknown is a person or what happened, then retry the choice. Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft usage scenario.** One person asks row 2 after leaving a room. The other reports row 1 or row 3 depending on what happened. Later a sound prompts row 4 in a different exchange. Check the turns with speakers in the intended context before using them as learner-facing models; no frequency or distribution is inferred.

## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** understanding whether a person, thing or event is in question.
- **Helpful.** familiar arrival and event predicates.
- **Co-taught.** negation and polarity interpretation.
- **Extension.** quantity modification and scope.

For oral activities, supply enough context through audio or the scene so Devanagari literacy is not an assessment prerequisite.
