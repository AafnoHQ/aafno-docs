## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Distinguish a count from a position.
- LO2: Recognise conventional ordinal forms.
- LO3: Interpret position in different sequences.
- LO4: Produce an ordinal expression with supplied vocabulary.

## 3. Meaning and usage

An ordinal locates an item in a sequence or ranking. The reference sequence determines what ‘first’ or ‘second’ means. A number of days and a position in a schedule answer different questions.

## 4. Formation rules

Set out a three-day schedule and compare a three-day count with the third day. Select the reviewed lexical form for the position and place it with the reference noun. Compare familiar forms before making any morphological generalisation.

**Worked models.** तीन दिन / Tin din / Three days (Count across a span.) Compare तेस्रो दिन / Tesro din / The third day (One position within an established schedule.)

## 5. Variations and agreement

Conventional shapes such as पहिलो / Pahilo / first and दोस्रो / Dosro / second should be reviewed alongside potentially productive patterns. Do not apply one suffix to every cardinal. Review agreement and floor or ranking conventions where they affect interpretation.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

A quantity does not answer which occurrence was meant. Keep the schedule visible until the difference is clear.

**Example corrective retry.** Draw three boxes and distinguish all boxes from only the third. Retry with a floor plan. Use [variant-aware assessment](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and potentially valid but unreviewed alternatives.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scenario.** Talk through a trip itinerary, choosing a day in the itinerary before asking which floor or visit is meant. Use the appropriate contextual models from section 6 for the response. Review naturalness and pronunciation before using this exchange as learner-facing material.

### LO2 — Recognise conventional ordinal forms

- **Learner prompt.** From the Nepali modifiers in rows 3 and 4, identify the ordinal for position two. Keep the ordinal glosses hidden and supply noun glosses separately.
- **Expected response.** दोस्रो / Dosro / second.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Score position and ordinal use; supply the conventional words when evaluating construction rather than recall. General derivational rules remain with G28.
- **Feedback and retry.** Separate the reference noun from the rank word; repeat with the expression for third place.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.

### LO3 — Interpret position in different sequences

- **Learner prompt.** Read तेस्रो दिन / Tesro din with the ordinal gloss hidden. On a supplied schedule starting Monday, choose the date the phrase refers to.
- **Expected response.** Wednesday, the third listed day.
- **Assessment target.** LO3 only. Score position and ordinal use; supply the conventional words when evaluating construction rather than recall. General derivational rules remain with G28.
- **Feedback and retry.** Point to each position on the schedule, then retry with a different starting day.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.

### LO4 — Produce an ordinal expression with supplied vocabulary

- **Learner prompt.** Give the Nepali phrase for the first occurrence. Supply the word for occurrence and the relevant vocabulary on separate cards, with the combined phrase hidden.
- **Expected response.** पहिलो पटक / Pahilo patak / first time.
- **Assessment target.** LO4 only. Score position and ordinal use; supply the conventional words when evaluating construction rather than recall. General derivational rules remain with G28.
- **Feedback and retry.** If you gave a count, return to the order of occurrences and combine the supplied ordinal and noun.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, interpretation, scoring limits and acceptable contextual alternatives before use.
