## 3. Meaning and usage

Quantifiers can express amount, subset, totality or sufficiency relative to a scene. Many and few have no universal thresholds, and a lexical form can contribute quantity in one context and degree or reference in another.

## 4. Formation rules

Choose an entity or substance first. Compare the book and water examples using the same form. Contrast an adjective target and a standalone indefinite. For ‘all’, establish the set first. For sufficient, specify a goal that fixes what is enough.

**Worked models.** धेरै किताब / धेरै पानी / Dherai kitaab / Dherai paani / Many books / a lot of water (Number of entities versus amount of substance.) Compare थोरै पानी / Thorai paani / A little water (Small amount relative to the task.)

## 5. Variations and agreement

Review count and mass frames, placement, sufficiency and possible collective or distributive readings. Do not assume every example belongs to one homogeneous determiner class. A quantity word with an activity may express extent, requiring contextual diagnosis.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

Point to the host expression before assigning an English meaning. A quality reading and a noun amount should be diagnosed separately.

**Example corrective retry.** If you confuse ‘some’ and ‘all’, mark the whole set and the unspecified subset. Compare the different recipe amounts, then try the match again with a new inventory. Use [variant-aware assessment](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and potentially valid but unreviewed alternatives.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scenario.** Ask whether the water is sufficient for a recipe, then discuss books and a quality evaluation using separate scenarios. ## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** A named set, substance or property in a situation.
- **Helpful.** Familiarity with the scenario vocabulary.
- **Co-taught.** Use the contrast in the guided example. Supply values, number words, clocks, labels or totals whenever they are supporting information.
- **Extension.** Refer to related canonical targets for independent evidence. Provide sufficient audio or scene cues for learners working without Devanagari.

- **Learner prompt.** Show rows 1–4 with the quantifier glosses hidden. Match the Nepali expressions to a large stock, a small amount, an unspecified subset and the entire class. Supply noun glosses only.
- **Expected response.** Use the many, few or little, some, and all examples respectively. The size of the stock or small amount is relative to the supplied situation.
- **Assessment target.** LO1 only. Score amount or contextual function in the noun phrase. G05-005 owns degree, G02-010 owns standalone indefinite reference and G03-007 owns countability.
