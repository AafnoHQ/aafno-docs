## 3. Meaning and usage

Grammatical functions describe how expressions fit the clause. Semantic roles describe their part in the event or state. The predicates and participants of a transfer and an experience must be analysed in context.

## 4. Formation rules

Identify the predicate first. Locate expressions that name participants or complete the predicate and distinguish expressions that add circumstances. A word, phrase or case-marked expression may realise a function. The subject can be inferred from discourse. In the transfer illustration, Ram is the agent and a candidate grammatical subject under the working analysis, Sita the recipient, the book the theme and candidate direct object, and ‘gave’ the verbal predicate. Review the syntactic analysis of the marked agent and recipient.

### Worked example

Bracket the transfer into four expressions. Annotate the marked giver as agent and candidate subject, the marked recipient as recipient with syntactic classification pending, the book as theme and candidate direct object, and the finite verb as predicate.

**Draft model.** पढ्दै छु / Padhdai chhu / am reading. In रामले सीतालाई किताब दियो। / Raamle Sitaalaai kitaab diyo. / Ram gave Sita a book, रामले / Raamle / Ram is the agent in the event; subject is a working analysis.

## 5. Variations and agreement

A predicate can include a descriptive complement and copula, or multiple verbal elements. A time phrase can modify the clause. Case and agreement can offer clues without proving the function in isolation. In the hunger example, the person is an experiencer. Its syntactic subject analysis remains open.

### Context and contrast

Compare the transfer with a hunger report and an ongoing reading report. The experiencer is a semantic role; reading plus auxiliary illustrates a multiword predicate. A time expression can modify the clause without filling a transfer role.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

If the learner labels every marked participant as a subject or every predicate as one word, replay the whole clause. Ask who did what and which words jointly communicate the action or state. Let the learner annotate role and function separately.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for reviewed alternatives, contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

**Targeted retry.** When the learner highlights only the last word of an ongoing reading predicate, replay the sentence and ask which words together express what is happening. Have them select the whole verbal expression.

## 9. Natural usage

Give a spoken question about what was given and a follow-up about how the listener felt. Ask the learner to identify the relevant expression and explain its function in everyday terms before introducing technical labels.

Draft exchange for review. Ask which expression answers the question and which other expressions belong to the predicate frame. Preserve the working syntactic analysis of the marked agent.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Required: recover the proposition.
- Helpful: know common case forms.
- Co-teach: clause annotation and role labels.
- Extension: disputed subject and complement analyses.

An understood sentence meaning, supplied by context or gloss as needed. Co-teach form/function labels and use everyday explanations before technical terminology. Requirements vary by outcome and depth. Use contextual audio and glosses for oral tasks so learners can demonstrate the target without script literacy.

## 12. Activity types

- Annotate a transfer at the phrase level.
- Use contextual listening to locate a full predicate.
- Compare an agent and an experiencer with separate role and function labels.

### Draft activity prototype

- **Prompt.** Mark the component that answers ‘what is happening now?’ in the reading example.
- **Expected response.** पढ्दै छु / Padhdai chhu / am reading. In रामले सीतालाई किताब दियो। / Raamle Sitaalaai kitaab diyo. / Ram gave Sita a book, रामले / Raamle / Ram is the agent in the event; subject is a working analysis.
- **Feedback.** Underline both predicate elements in the reading example. Unlike the agent label, the subject label requires syntactic analysis.

## 13. Mastery evidence

In unseen validated speech or text, select the full predicate and identify participant expressions and a modifier. Require contextual justification of at least one role–function distinction. A correct label inferred from a case marker alone is insufficient.

**Coverage check.** Require an independent annotation of a multiword predicate, a modifier, and at least one function–role contrast. Accept everyday explanations of the distinction.
