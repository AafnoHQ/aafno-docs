# Experiencer constructions

**Phase 2A.2 Step 3A specification draft.** Product-owner approval covers the pilot direction. Language examples and assessment alternatives await review. [Pilot index](index.md) · [Content QA](../content-qa.md).

## 1. Concept identity

- Catalogue ID: **G24-012** · [G24 — Participant structures, voice and agency](../grammar-catalogue/connecting-ideas.md#g24).
- English title: Experiencer constructions.
- Proposed Nepali terminology: अनुभवकर्ता संरचना.
- Romanisation: Anubhavkartaa sanrachanaa.
- Relevant marker: लाई — Laai.
- Concept type: Participant structure and predicate-specific syntax.
- Catalogue learning outcome: Interpret and express experiencer participant structures, including feelings, sensations and experiences. Identify and apply dative experiencer marking with लाई where appropriate.
- Specification status: Draft implementing the approved pilot direction.
- Validation status: Linguistic and native-speaker review pending. Publication eligibility unestablished.
- CEFR assignment: Unassigned.
- Reconciled source: G14-007.

## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise experiencer constructions.
- LO2: Identify the experiencer participant.
- LO3: Apply appropriate dative experiencer marking.
- LO4: Select suitable predicates for different experiences.
- LO5: Apply validated agreement patterns.
- LO6: Distinguish experiencers from agents, recipients and objects.
- LO7: Interpret and produce temporal, negative and question variations.
- LO8: Transfer established constructions to unfamiliar contexts.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Agentive example:

मैले खाना खाएँ।

Maile khaana khaaẽ.

I ate food.

Experiencer example:

मलाई भोक लाग्यो।

Malaai bhok laagyo.

I got hungry.

Preference:

मलाई यो मन पर्छ।

Malaai yo man parchha.

I like this.

Knowledge:

मलाई थाहा छ।

Malaai thaahaa chha.

I know.

The experiencer is the participant undergoing or experiencing the relevant state.

Do not imply every experience requires dative marking.

## 4. Formation rules

म — Ma — I.

मलाई — Malaai — Me, dative/object-marked.

Investigate predicate-specific construction families:

| Predicate | Illustrative function |
|---|---|
| लाग्नु — Laagnu | Sensations and feelings |
| मन पर्नु — Man parnu | Liking and preference |
| थाहा हुनु — Thaahaa hunu | Knowledge |
| दुख्नु — Dukhnu | Pain |
| याद आउनु — Yaad aaunu | Memory and missing |
| मन लाग्नु — Man laagnu | Desire or inclination |
| मन हुनु — Man hunu | Desire or inclination |

Do not assume the families are interchangeable.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Illustrative experiencer forms:

| Pronoun | Marked form |
|---|---|
| म — Ma | मलाई — Malaai |
| तँ — Ta | तँलाई — Talaai |
| तिमी — Timi | तिमीलाई — Timilaai |
| तपाईं — Tapaai | तपाईंलाई — Tapaailaai |
| हजुर — Hajur | हजुरलाई — Hajurlaai |
| ऊ — U | उसलाई — Uslaai |
| उनी — Uni | उनलाई — Unlaai |
| उहाँ — Uhã | उहाँलाई — Uhãlaai |
| हामी — Haami | हामीलाई — Haamilaai |
| उनीहरू — Uniharu | उनीहरूलाई — Uniharulaai |

These may be illustrated with भोक लाग्यो (Bhok laagyo), meaning the relevant experiencer got hungry.

Investigate the agreement controller for each predicate family.

Do not assume that the experiencer always controls agreement.

Do not assume that every experiencer construction has invariant agreement.

Temporal and polarity examples:

मलाई भोक लागेको छ।

Malaai bhok laageko chha.

I am hungry.

मलाई बिहान भोक लाग्छ।

Malaai bihaan bhok laagchha.

I get hungry in the morning.

मलाई भोक लाग्नेछ।

Malaai bhok laagnechha.

I will get hungry.

मलाई भोक लागेको छैन।

Malaai bhok laageko chhaina.

I am not hungry.

तिमीलाई भोक लाग्यो?

Timilaai bhok laagyo?

Did you get hungry?

Validate these constructions and their naturalness.

Do not mechanically apply highest-honorific conjugation to every experiencer predicate.

| Devanagari | Romanisation | English | Illustrative context | Analysis to review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| मलाई तिर्खा लाग्यो। | Malaai tirkhaa laagyo. | I got thirsty. | Report thirst after an activity. | Speaker as experiencer; thirst predicate and past onset. |
| मलाई टाउको दुखेको छ। | Malaai taauko dukheko chha. | I have a headache. | Describe a symptom in conversation. | Speaker as experiencer; head/body part and pain predicate. Review aspect and agreement. |
| मलाई डर लाग्यो। | Malaai dar laagyo. | I got scared. | Respond to something frightening. | Speaker as experiencer; fear predicate and past onset. |
| मलाई नेपाली खाना मन पर्छ। | Malaai Nepaali khaana man parchha. | I like Nepali food. | Discuss food preferences. | Speaker as experiencer and Nepali food as stimulus. Review the predicate’s agreement controller. |
| मलाई थाहा छैन। | Malaai thaahaa chhaina. | I do not know. | Respond to a question with uncertainty. | Speaker as experiencer; knowledge predicate with negative polarity. |
| मलाई घर जान मन लाग्यो। | Malaai ghar jaana man laagyo. | I felt like going home. | Recall wanting to leave. | Speaker as experiencer; desire predicate with a non-finite complement. |
| मलाई घरको याद आउँछ। | Malaai gharko yaad aauchha. | I miss home. | Discuss homesickness. | Speaker as experiencer; memory or longing predicate. Review agreement and habitual reading. |

Record experiencer, predicate family, other participants, agreement, context, register and validation status.

## 7. Related constructions and contrasts

Contrast:

म — Ma — I.

मैले — Maile — Agentive-marked I.

मलाई — Malaai — Dative/object-marked me.

Recipient:

दाइले मलाई किताब दिनुभयो।

Daaile malaai kitaab dinubhayo.

Older brother gave me a book.

Object:

उसले मलाई देख्यो।

Usle malaai dekhyo.

He saw me.

The same लाई-marked form can express different participant relationships.

Cross-reference [G14-003](agentive-and-ergative-marking.md), G14-005, G14-006, G14-024, G14-027, G24-001, G24-011, G24-015 and [G01-004](subject-omission.md) where relevant.

G24-012 is the canonical experiencer target.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

Address:

- Literal translation from English.
- Confusing agentive and experiencer marking.
- Treating every लाई-marked participant as an experiencer.
- Incorrect agreement.
- Incorrect predicate selection.
- Assuming every emotion requires dative marking.
- Mechanical honorific inflection.

**Feedback and retry.** Ask who experiences the feeling and what other participant or predicate is present. Have the learner identify what controls agreement in the reviewed construction, then retry with a different experience of the same type.

## 9. Natural usage

तिमीलाई भोक लाग्यो?

Timilaai bhok laagyo?

Did you get hungry?

अँ, धेरै भोक लाग्यो।

Aã, dherai bhok laagyo.

Yes, I got very hungry.

के खान मन छ?

Ke khaana man chha?

What do you feel like eating?

मलाई मःमः खान मन छ।

Malaai momo khaana man chha.

I feel like eating momo.

चिया पनि खाने?

Chiya pani khaane?

Tea as well?

अहिले चिया खान मन छैन।

Ahile chiya khaana man chhaina.

I do not feel like drinking tea right now.

An omitted experiencer is not automatically an instance of ordinary subject omission.

Investigate advanced argument omission separately.

Compare:

मलाई घर जान मन छ।

Malaai ghar jaana man chha.

I feel like going home.

मलाई घर जान मन लाग्यो।

Malaai ghar jaana man laagyo.

I felt like going home.

म घर जान चाहन्छु।

Ma ghar jaana chaahanchhu.

I want to go home.

Validate the overlap and differences in meaning.

## 10. Prerequisites

Pronouns and participant-role knowledge are helpful.

Dative marking may be co-taught.

Relevant vocabulary is required.

Agreement becomes important for independent production.

Negation, questions and register depend on the task.

Devanagari literacy is not required.

## 11. Teaching depth

| Teaching depth | Outcomes | Observable performance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | LO1, LO2, LO4 | Recognise who feels something and match the experience to a predicate. |
| Guided application | LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6, LO7 | Use predicate and participant cues to construct questions, negatives and affirmative responses. |
| Independent application | LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6, LO7, LO8 | Select a predicate and produce the construction without a sentence frame. |
| Flexible application | LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6, LO7, LO8 | Transfer across unfamiliar validated experiences and distinguish participant roles in context. |

Introduce practical everyday expressions early.

## 12. Activity types

Include:

- Identifying an experiencer in a preference statement.
- Describing someone feeling cold.
- Distinguishing a recipient from an experiencer.
- Saying that something is unknown.
- Listening.
- Predicate selection.
- Case correction.
- Transformation.
- Spoken family roleplay.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Assess:

- Recognition.
- Participant identification.
- Case marking.
- Predicate selection.
- Agreement.
- Participant-role distinction.
- Production.
- Transfer.

Independent task: visit a relative and communicate hunger, food preferences, drink preferences, whether someone is cold and whether something is known.

Valid alternative expressions may receive communicative credit without establishing mastery of the targeted experiencer construction.
