## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Choose a person, entity or choice form.
- LO2: Identify the information requested.
- LO3: Distinguish standalone and noun-modifying uses.
- LO4: Distinguish person questions from place questions.
- LO5: Recover the requested role in a case-marked example.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Ask for an identity or a selection among alternatives. English glosses do not map one-to-one onto the forms.

## 4. Formation rules

Start with a missing participant. Row 1 asks for a person, row 2 for the identity or type of a thing and row 3 for selection among known items. Row 4 narrows the set by naming its category.

**Worked models.** त्यो को हो? / Tyo ko ho? / Who is that? Compare यो के हो? / Yo ke ho? / What is this? Use the situations in section 6 to establish the intended readings.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Choice sets can be supplied by context. Case-marked variants signal a role in the predicate; time and place questions belong to G17.

## 7. Related constructions and contrasts

G17 owns question construction and interrogative adverbs. G14 owns case transformations. Distinguish a requested identity from a referent constrained by a relative expression.

Related canonical targets: [G17](../grammar-catalogue/relationships-and-transformations.md#g17), [G14](../grammar-catalogue/relationships-and-transformations.md#g14), [G02-005](demonstrative-pronouns.md), [G02-011](relative-pronouns.md).

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Likely misunderstanding.** Translating every English ‘what’ as one form can obscure the difference between naming an unknown item and choosing among known options.

**Retry.** Make both options visible; ask whether the listener must identify an unknown thing or choose an option. For the contrast target, locate the item given, then identify which part of the transfer remains unknown. Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft usage scenario.** A visitor first sees a new tool and asks row 2. After the seller explains the tools, the visitor hears row 3 and understands they must choose from that known set. Check the turns with speakers in the intended context before using them as learner-facing models; no frequency or distribution is inferred.

## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** a comprehensible situation with an unknown slot.
- **Helpful.** familiar participant and entity vocabulary.
- **Co-taught.** identity and selection cues.
- **Extension.** case-marked and broader question forms.

For oral activities, supply enough context through audio or the scene so Devanagari literacy is not an assessment prerequisite.

## 12. Activity types

Row references below refer to the contextual examples in section 6. Use all three language representations by default; adjust visibility only for the evidence target.

### Initial target

- **Learner prompt.** Choose the question for selecting one of two tools.
- **Expected response.** Row 3.
- **Assessment target.** LO1. Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** Make both options visible; ask whether the listener must identify an unknown thing or choose an option.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

### Contrast target

- **Learner prompt.** What information does row 5 ask for?
- **Expected response.** The recipient, not the book or the destination.
- **Assessment target.** LO5. Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** Locate the item given, then identify which part of the transfer remains unknown.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Use fresh person, entity, selection and place questions plus one recipient example. Elicit form choice and requested information independently. Record what the answer demonstrates against the local outcomes and keep stem, ending, case, agreement and related semantic evidence separate. Guided performance can inform feedback; mastery requires an independent task and flexible evidence where relevant.
