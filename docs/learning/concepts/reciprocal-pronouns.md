## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise the reciprocal form.
- LO2: Map participants in a mutual action.
- LO3: Distinguish mutual, self-directed, parallel and third-party actions.
- LO4: Interpret reciprocity in larger groups.
- LO5: Produce a reviewed reciprocal report.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Reciprocity describes action directed between members of a group. A pair can act toward each other without acting on themselves; a larger group need not describe every possible pair.

## 4. Formation rules

Map an action arrow from each of two colleagues to the other. Compare a self-loop, parallel independent actions and arrows to a third party. In the first example, the reciprocal expression picks out other participants within the group.

**Worked models.** उनीहरू एकअर्कालाई मद्दत गर्छन्। / Uniharu ekaarkaalaai maddat garchhan. / They help each other. Compare उनीहरू आफैँ काम गर्छन्। / Uniharu aafaĩ kaam garchhan. / They do the work themselves. Use the situations in section 6 to establish the intended readings.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Case and reciprocal possession use related mechanisms owned by G14 and G15. Group size and interaction structure require context; do not infer a complete network just from the expression.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Likely misunderstanding.** A learner may mistake several people acting in parallel for a mutual relationship.

**Retry.** If both arrows go to Ram, compare row 3 and rebuild the mutual scene. For the contrast target, remove one possible link in the diagram and separate what is stated from what the scene supplies. Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft usage scenario.** Two coworkers describe duties using row 2 and later describe mutual help using row 1. A third listener checks whether the help was within the pair or directed to Ram as in row 3. Check the turns with speakers in the intended context before using them as learner-facing models; no frequency or distribution is inferred.

## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** a clear account of who acts toward whom.
- **Helpful.** familiar group pronouns.
- **Co-taught.** use of interaction diagrams.
- **Extension.** case and reciprocal possession.

For oral activities, supply enough context through audio or the scene so Devanagari literacy is not an assessment prerequisite.

## 12. Activity types

Row references below refer to the contextual examples in section 6. Use all three language representations by default; adjust visibility only for the evidence target.

### Initial target

- **Learner prompt.** Each colleague helps the other. Draw the arrows and give a report.
- **Expected response.** Two arrows between the colleagues; say row 1.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 and LO5. Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** If both arrows go to Ram, compare row 3 and rebuild the mutual scene.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

### Contrast target

- **Learner prompt.** With three colleagues, does the report tell us exactly which pairs helped each other?
- **Expected response.** It does not specify every interaction in this draft scenario.
- **Assessment target.** LO4. Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** Remove one possible link in the diagram and separate what is stated from what the scene supplies.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

## 13. Mastery evidence
