## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise a quality word in context.
- LO2: Identify the entity described.
- LO3: Choose a word for an observed property.
- LO4: Use a description to distinguish two entities.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A description can convey size, age, appearance, taste or an evaluation. Ask what property matters in the conversation and which entity has it. Good or nice is more context-dependent than large.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare a room’s size with its appearance. To identify a particular room, combine the target noun with a useful quality; to report a quality about a known room, use a reviewed predicate frame. Leave arrangement and inflection to their owners.

**Worked models.** ठूलो कोठा / Thulo kothaa / A large room (A visitor wants space for a group.) Compare सानो कोठा / Saano kothaa / A small room (Compare the same noun with a different size.) The examples are instructional candidates.

## 5. Variations and agreement

A word can be descriptive in one construction and serve another function elsewhere. Do not infer class, gender or position from meaning alone. Taste terms can have overlapping readings.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

A learner can sound plausible while using a cue unrelated to the target. Test what they inferred with the two independent prompts in section 12.

**Corrective cue for the first prompt.** Underline the noun and compare the two options before retrying.

**Corrective cue for the second prompt.** If you chose a word for appearance, compare the room sizes and choose the property requested.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) if context permits more than one answer or the variant has not been reviewed.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scenario.** A visitor asks which room can fit the group. Answer with the large-room label, then compare an evaluation used in a home viewing. Use the utterances from section 6 in the specified turns. Validate their naturalness in the exchange before using them with learners.

## 12. Activity types

References to rows are to section 6. Provide Devanagari, Romanisation and English by default. For unaided production, hide examples and target expressions until after the response. A scaffolded response is recorded as guided practice.

### First evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** In the room pair, which entity does the description refer to?
- **Expected response.** The room is described.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Noun selection and agreement are supporting evidence only.
- **Feedback and retry.** Underline the noun and compare the two options before retrying.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.

### Second evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** Two available rooms differ only in size. Ask for the large room in a short Nepali phrase without the model.
- **Expected response.** ठूलो कोठा / Thulo kothaa / a large room.
- **Assessment target.** LO4 only. Noun selection and agreement are supporting evidence only.
- **Feedback and retry.** If you chose a word for appearance, compare the room sizes and choose the property requested.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.
