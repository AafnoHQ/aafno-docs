## 3. Meaning and usage

A transfer can involve a source, something transferred and a recipient. The roles follow from the meaning and verbal frame in context. The syntactic classification of an expression and its case marking are separate analyses.

## 4. Formation rules

Use the Ram, Sita and book example to trace the book from giver to recipient. Compare a perception or reading example with three visible scene details but no transfer. Finally hold the book and recipient recoverable while a follow-up names only the giver.

**Worked illustrations.** रामले सीतालाई किताब दियो। / Raamle Sitaalaai kitaab diyo. / Ram gave Sita a book. (Source Ram, theme book, recipient Sita; subject analysis remains provisional.) Compare रामले किताब पढ्यो। / Raamle kitaab padhyo. / Ram read a book. (Object involvement without a receiving event.)

## 5. Variations and agreement

Compare giving, sending, showing and telling as candidate object or information transfers. Their syntactic patterns need not be identical. Do not use the number of expressions or a suffix alone to classify a construction.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Diagnose the interpretation.** Trace whether something passes to someone, then retry with a reported information transfer.

**Retry without leaking the target.** Follow the book from its source to its recipient and retry with reversed participants. Use [variant-aware assessment](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) when a plausible response depends on a reading or form still under review.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scene.** Two people discuss a book intended for Sita. After someone gives it, use the full report and the shorter follow-up to check what a listener can reasonably recover. Use the matching contextual utterances in section 6 and review their naturalness in the exchange.
