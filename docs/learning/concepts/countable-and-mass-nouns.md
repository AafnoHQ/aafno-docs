## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Identify direct entity counts.
- LO2: Identify what a measure or container quantifies.
- LO3: Distinguish substance from portion or container.
- LO4: Select a quantity unit for the task.
- LO5: Explain when a portion or type reading depends on context.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Some quantities count individuals; others measure material or count containers or servings. Context determines the relevant unit.

## 4. Formation rules

Count apples in a shop, measure water for a recipe and count glasses of milk when serving. The numeral in the milk example counts glasses or servings in context, rather than separate instances of milk as a substance.

**Worked models.** दुईवटा स्याउ / Duiwata syaau / Two apples Compare दुई लिटर पानी / Dui litar paani / Two litres of water Use the situations in section 6 to establish the intended readings.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Substances may be construed as portions or kinds. Validate short ordering expressions and do not import English countability restrictions as Nepali rules.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Likely misunderstanding.** A learner may count units that do not match the practical purpose, such as bottles instead of litres.

**Retry.** If you count waters or bottles, clarify whether the recipe specifies volume or containers. For the contrast target, underline the unit and compare what changes if you measure the same substance by volume. Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft usage scenario.** A shopper uses row 1, then a cook requests row 2. In a café, compare row 3 and ask whether a shorter request would still make the serving unit clear. Check the turns with speakers in the intended context before using them as learner-facing models; no frequency or distribution is inferred.

## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** a practical purpose such as counting stock or measuring ingredients.
- **Helpful.** familiar unit vocabulary.
- **Co-taught.** entity versus serving contrasts.
- **Extension.** detailed classifiers and alternate type readings.

For oral activities, supply enough context through audio or the scene so Devanagari literacy is not an assessment prerequisite.
