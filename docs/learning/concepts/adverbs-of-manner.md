## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Identify what action the expression modifies.
- LO2: Interpret how the action is done.
- LO3: Distinguish manner from speed as timing or a quality description.
- LO4: Produce an appropriate manner expression.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Manner describes how an action is carried out, such as speaking slowly or doing something carefully. A similar form can describe an entity or timing in another construction.

## 4. Formation rules

Keep speaking fixed and swap its manner. Compare slow delivery and good delivery, then apply a carefully expression to a different action. Check which interpretation changes rather than translating each form in isolation.

**Worked models.** ऊ बिस्तारै बोल्छ। / U bistaarai bolchha. / He or she speaks slowly. (Manner is slow speech delivery.) Compare ऊ राम्ररी बोल्छ। / U raamrari bolchha. / He or she speaks well. (Quality of speaking, not how often it happens.) The examples are instructional candidates.

## 5. Variations and agreement

A speed word may suggest quick action, early timing or a descriptive quality depending on context. Review contextual function; derivation and placement have separate owners.

Store each reviewed manner expression with its context and structure. The English suffix ‘-ly’ is not a model for generating a Nepali form. Relate detailed derivation to G28 only where the source supports it.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

A learner can sound plausible while using a cue unrelated to the target. Test what they inferred with the two independent prompts in section 12.

**Corrective cue for the first prompt.** Ask how the person speaks and point to that action before retrying.

**Corrective cue for the second prompt.** If you described when they speak, replay a short and slow recording and describe the delivery instead.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) if context permits more than one answer or the variant has not been reviewed.

References to rows are to section 6. Provide Devanagari, Romanisation and English by default. For unaided production, hide examples and target expressions until after the response. A scaffolded response is recorded as guided practice.
