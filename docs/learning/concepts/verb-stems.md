## 3. Meaning and usage

A root is an analytical lexical base, a stem is the base used in a given formation, and a surface form is the realised spoken or written result. A stem may have alternative shapes across constructions. Keep these distinctions where they clarify form relationships.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare पढ्नु / Padhnu / to read with पढ् / Padh / read stem and पढ्छु / Padhchhu / I read. Then compare a vowel-final candidate. A written consonant sign can make the candidate base clearer without proving a complete analysis.

**Worked illustrations.** गर्नु / गर् / Garnu / Gar / To do / candidate do stem (Consonant-final comparison.) Compare खानु / खा / Khaanu / Khaa / To eat / candidate eat stem (Vowel-final comparison.)

## 5. Variations and agreement

Do not analyse every surface string by just removing नु / Nu / infinitive ending. Spelling and sound can obscure the boundary, and a correspondence may require an alternate stem or an exceptional relation. Avoid forcing a unique root when the evidence supports only a useful stem comparison. Relevant validated register comparisons may eventually extend this work. [G08-005](highest-honorific-conjugation-and-agreement.md) owns highest-honorific conjugation and agreement. G12-004, G25-005 and G31-007 retain imperative formation, social selection and family usage. Do not derive family forms from standard respectful forms.

**Diagnose the interpretation.** Mark what changes between the entry and a compared form, then try the same segmentation with बोल्नु / Bolnu / to speak.

**Retry without leaking the target.** Compare the start of both forms before identifying the ending. Retry with a supplied speak pair. Use [variant-aware assessment](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) when a plausible response depends on a reading or form still under review.
