## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise inherent and explicitly marked plural personal forms.
- LO2: Classify first-, second- and third-person group reference.
- LO3: Distinguish actual plural reference from respectful singular agreement.
- LO4: Identify explicit हरू in reviewed pronoun forms.
- LO5: Interpret plurality when it depends on context.
- LO6: Choose a suitable group pronoun in familiar and unfamiliar contexts.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Plural personal pronouns refer to groups in the speech situation. Identify whether the group includes the speaker, is being addressed, or is being discussed. Distinguish inherent plurality, explicit marking and plurality understood from context.

## 4. Formation rules

Use हामी / Haami / we as an inherently plural starting form. Compare forms with हरू / Haru / plural, including तिमीहरू / Timiharu / you (plural), तपाईंहरू / Tapaaiharu / you (respectful plural), उनीहरू / Uniharu / they, and उहाँहरू / Uhãharu / they (respectful). Teach reviewed form combinations individually and explain when explicit marking is appropriate.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Context may clarify number without an explicit marker. Honorific agreement does not reliably count people. Review optionality, family variation, group register and case or possessive transformations through G14 and G15. Keep predicate agreement separate.

| हामी फुटबल खेल्छौँ। | Haami football khelchhaũ. | We play football. | Speak on behalf of the speaker’s team. |

| उनीहरू घर जान्छन्। | Uniharu ghar jaanchhan. | They are going home. | Talk about a group already identified. |
| उहाँ काम गर्नुहुन्छ। | Uhã kaam garnuhunchha. | He or she works, respectfully. | Context specifies one referent despite respectful agreement. |
| उहाँहरू काम गर्नुहुन्छ। | Uhãharu kaam garnuhunchha. | They work, respectfully. | Context specifies several referents; compare the draft agreement with the singular example. |

## 7. Related constructions and contrasts

Distinguish actual group reference, formal plural marking and agreement that occurs in respectful address. Assess group pronoun choice independently of agreement and case.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

If the learner counts people from the verb ending alone, replay the singular and plural respectful pair and point them to discourse and pronoun marking. If they select the wrong person group, ask who speaks, who listens and who is discussed.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for reviewed alternatives, contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

Use a team organiser scenario: speak as the team, ask a group about plans, then report about people who are absent. Contrast addressing one person respectfully with addressing the whole group.

Draft exchange for review. One member answers for the group. The same people are a second-person group from the questioner's perspective and a first-person group from the respondent's perspective.

| Turn | Devanagari | Romanisation | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Question | तपाईंहरू कहाँ जानुहुन्छ? | Tapaaiharu kahaã jaanuhunchha? | Where are you all going? |
| Reply | हामी घर जान्छौँ। | Haami ghar jaanchhaũ. | We’re going home. |

## 10. Prerequisites

Basic speech-participant roles and contextual number. Co-teach reviewed group forms and comparison with singular respect. Extend to case, possession and household variants as evidence becomes available. Requirements vary by outcome and depth. Use contextual audio and glosses for oral tasks so learners can demonstrate the target without script literacy.

## 12. Activity types

- Sort speaker groups, addressee groups and third-person groups.
- Compare respectful singular and plural examples.
- Address a group and report on a different group independently.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Switch among three unfamiliar groups and one respectful singular referent. Require an interpretation of person and actual number plus original group reference. Credit case, suffix formation beyond reviewed target forms and agreement only when separately assessed.
