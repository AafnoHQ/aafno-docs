## 3. Meaning and usage

Valency concerns which participants and complements a predicate meaning and construction require or permit. Semantic participants and spelled-out expressions are distinct. An optional expression is not automatically an adjunct, and a meaningful participant may remain unspoken.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare sleeper, eater plus food and giver plus item plus recipient. Keep the same meal predicate while withholding an overt object. Add a time expression to the sleep report and identify its contribution. Compare the destination with the event frame before deciding whether it is selected or supplementary.

**Worked illustrations.** बच्चा सुत्छ। / Bachchaa sutchha. / The child sleeps. (Sleeper frame.) Compare बच्चा आज सुत्छ। / Bachchaa aaja sutchha. / The child sleeps today. (Time supplied beyond the core participant.)

## 5. Variations and agreement

Use one frame at a time. Explore how alternate meanings or constructions change participant expectations, and how discourse can recover something without changing the frame. More complex changes remain with their owners.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Diagnose the interpretation.** Match the conceptual slots to the predicates before counting words; retry with the eating frame.

**Retry without leaking the target.** Place the time information on a timeline rather than in the participant slot; retry with a different time cue. Use [variant-aware assessment](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) when a plausible response depends on a reading or form still under review.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft conversational scene.** Compare a sleep report with a time cue, a brief reply about eating offered rice and an account of giving a book. Ask what the listener needs to infer and what the predicate leaves open. Use the matching contextual utterances in section 6 and review their naturalness in the exchange.

### LO2 — Distinguish a selected participant from added circumstantial information

- **Learner prompt.** What does आज / Aaja / today add to the Nepali sleep report, and is it a participant sleeping?
- **Expected response.** It situates the event in time. The child is the sleeper.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Assess the supported frame, supported omission and limits of inference. Keep whole-clause labels, transitivity contrasts, case and adverbial meaning with their existing owners.
- **Feedback and retry.** Place the time information on a timeline rather than in the participant slot; retry with a different time cue.

### LO3 — Interpret a contextually recoverable omitted participant

- **Learner prompt.** After a rice offer, read म खान्छु। / Ma khaanchhu. Does the missing food noun necessarily remove the food from the interpreted frame?
- **Expected response.** No. The offered rice remains recoverable in this context.
- **Assessment target.** LO3 only. Assess the supported frame, supported omission and limits of inference. Keep whole-clause labels, transitivity contrasts, case and adverbial meaning with their existing owners.
- **Feedback and retry.** Replay the offer and mark the implicit slot; retry with a different food.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm the example, analysis and accepted responses before use.

### LO4 — Compare alternative uses without counting visible phrases

- **Learner prompt.** Compare बच्चाहरू खेल्छन्। / Bachchaaharu khelchhan. in a brief playground report with बच्चाहरू फुटबल खेल्छन्। / Bachchaaharu football khelchhan. in a report about a football session. Hide the full glosses. How do these uses differ in what the listener can recover about the activity?
- **Expected response.** The first states that the children play but leaves the activity unspecified in this scene. The second names football. The difference supports comparing uses; it does not settle whether the first is syntactically a distinct frame or omits an object.
- **Assessment target.** LO4 only. Assess the supported frame, supported omission and limits of inference. Keep whole-clause labels, transitivity contrasts, case and adverbial meaning with their existing owners.
- **Feedback and retry.** Identify the activity named by the second use, then check whether the first scene supplies any identifiable activity. Retry with a new scene that does make the activity recoverable.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm the example, analysis and accepted responses before use.

### LO5 — Identify when evidence about required or permitted complements is insufficient

- **Learner prompt.** Read म घर जान्छु। / Ma ghar jaanchhu. with a lexical gloss for home and go. This movement report supplies a destination. Does this example alone establish that the predicate always requires one to be expressed?
- **Expected response.** No. Test whether the frame is complete without it in suitable contexts and compare other destinations and circumstances before deciding.
- **Assessment target.** LO5 only. Assess the supported frame, supported omission and limits of inference. Keep whole-clause labels, transitivity contrasts, case and adverbial meaning with their existing owners.
- **Feedback and retry.** Ask what the one example establishes, then request a matched context with an omitted destination.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm the example, analysis and accepted responses before use.

### LO6 — Distinguish participant requirements from overt expression

- **Learner prompt.** Compare बच्चा सुत्छ। / Bachchaa sutchha., बच्चा आज सुत्छ। / Bachchaa aaja sutchha., and सुत्छ। / Sutchha. as a short reply after a question about that child. Give lexical glosses only. In this supported sleep frame, which participant is necessary to interpret the event, which extra expression is permitted, and which participant need not be spoken in the short reply?
- **Expected response.** There must be a sleeper in the interpreted event. आज / Aaja / today is a permitted time expression in the second report. The known child can be recovered without an overt noun in the short reply. If the context does not identify a child, request a clarifying expression rather than claiming that a noun is always grammatically obligatory.
- **Assessment target.** LO6 only. Score required participant, permitted overt expression and recoverable omission separately within the supplied frame. G01-004 owns subject omission as a construction and G05 owns temporal meaning.
- **Feedback and retry.** If the learner says no overt noun means no sleeper, replay the prior question. Retry with the sleeper established and then with two eligible sleepers. Ask when clarification is needed.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the short reply, the exact contexts that support omission, and any claims of obligatory expression separately.

### LO7 — Apply a supplied frame to an unfamiliar context

- **Learner prompt.** Supply the reviewed giving frame with three slots: giver, transferred item and recipient. In a new dialogue, the interlocutors have just established that a particular pen is intended for Maya. Then the learner hears गीताले कलम दिइन्। / Gitaale kalam diin. with lexical glosses only. Map overt and recoverable information onto the supplied frame, and say what would remain unknown if the first turn were removed.
- **Expected response.** Gita is the giver and the pen is the item. Maya is the intended recipient only if the preceding turn establishes that the pen was for her. If the actual lead-in leaves more than one recipient plausible, identify Maya as a candidate and request clarification. With no prior turn, the recipient is unspecified.
- **Assessment target.** LO7 only. Score applying the supplied frame to the unfamiliar report, including limits on recovering a missing slot. Do not score case, agreement or whole-clause component labels.
- **Feedback and retry.** If the learner drops the recipient slot or treats Maya as certain from the verb alone, revisit which roles the frame makes relevant and which the discourse establishes. Retry with a reviewed transfer and explicit prior recipient cue.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Validate the example’s agreement and naturalness, and establish which lead-in is sufficient for participant recovery before use.
