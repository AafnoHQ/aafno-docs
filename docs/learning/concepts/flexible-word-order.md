# Flexible word order

**Phase 2A.2 Step 3B Batch 1 specification draft.** [Batch register](../specification-batches.md#step-3b-batch-1) · [Content QA](../content-qa.md).

- Concept type: Contextual interpretation of constituent order.
- Catalogue learning outcome: Recognise how word order changes without necessarily changing the core meaning.
- Specification status: Draft.
- Validation status: Linguistic and native-speaker review pending. Publication eligibility unestablished.
- CEFR assignment: Unassigned.

## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise the common foundational arrangement.
- LO2: Identify reviewed alternative orders in context.
- LO3: Recover who did what across matched orders.
- LO4: Distinguish preserved core meaning from a changed meaning or unresolved reading.
- LO5: Explain why context or emphasis can affect suitability.
- LO6: Interpret an unfamiliar reviewed arrangement using several cues.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Some changes in constituent order can preserve the core proposition while shifting what listeners take to be topical or contrastive. With other changes, interpretation may become ambiguous or different. Specific information-structure functions belong to G29-006.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare two versions with the same words and predicate. Keep participant and time readings fixed where the context supports them. Identify case, agreement, predicate meaning, discourse and prosody as possible evidence. Review whether the object-first candidate fits the intended context before using it as a model.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Predicate position, modifier scope, omission and ambiguous unmarked participants can change the result. Case cues do not resolve every ambiguity, and even a clearly recoverable proposition may sound unsuitable in a particular context or register.

## 7. Related constructions and contrasts

Related canonical targets: [G01-001](../grammar-catalogue/grammar-foundations.md#g01), [G01-004](../grammar-catalogue/grammar-foundations.md#g01), [G14](../grammar-catalogue/relationships-and-transformations.md#g14), [G29-006](../grammar-catalogue/honorifics-naturalness-and-advanced-language.md#g29).

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

If the learner rejects every departure from SOV, ask whether roles and circumstances remain recoverable in the reviewed context. If they freely reorder words, require a paraphrase and check modifier scope. Have them retry in the context that supports the target reading.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for reviewed alternatives, contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

Play the baseline and candidate orders after a broad question and after a focused question. Ask what stays the same, what becomes prominent and where a listener might need clarification.

Draft exchange for review. The question has already put rice under discussion. Review the object-first reply with native-speaker judgments and prosody before presenting it as an accepted alternative.

| Turn | Devanagari | Romanisation | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Question | भात कसले खान्छ? | Bhaat kasle khaanchha? | Who eats rice? |
| Reply | भात म खान्छु। | Bhaat ma khaanchhu. | I eat rice, with rice already under discussion. |

## 10. Prerequisites

An understood baseline clause and its participants. Co-teach marking and context clues. Sustained discourse and focus production belong to a later extension. Requirements vary by outcome and depth. Use contextual audio and glosses for oral tasks so learners can demonstrate the target without script literacy.

## 12. Activity types

- Match alternative orders to a spoken context.
- Test core meaning with paraphrases and changed-scope foils.
- Use a new pair and ask for an interpretation with evidence.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Use a new pair and a new potentially ambiguous arrangement. Ask for who did what and whether the alternatives are equivalent for the intended context. Credit initial construction, discourse focus and flexible-order interpretation only against their own outcomes.
