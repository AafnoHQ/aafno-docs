## 3. Meaning and usage

A dependent clause gives a circumstance or complement that relates to the main message. A condition can determine when a plan applies, a reason can explain it, and time can situate it. A phrase conveying time may serve a similar communicative purpose without the same clause structure.

## 4. Formation rules

Identify the main proposition, formulate the dependent content and choose a reviewed linking frame. In the rain illustration, the rain clause sets a condition and staying home is the consequence. Read the past-looking form in the full conditional context and review its analysis with the canonical conditional concepts.

### Worked example

Take ‘I will stay at home’ as the main plan and add ‘if it rains’ to condition it. For the causal example, map rain to the reason for a past decision. For the time example, map finishing work to when departure follows.

**Draft model.** पानी पर्‍यो भने म घरमै बस्छु। / Paani paryo bhane ma gharmai baschhu. / If it rains, I will stay at home. पानी परेकाले म घरमै बसेँ। / Paani parekaale ma gharmai basẽ. / I stayed at home because it rained.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Review finite and non-finite boundaries, ordering, temporal reference, agreement and possible omission. Compare a reported-speech complement or advanced non-finite construction only as a boundary case. A change of order or connector may change interpretation.

### Context and contrast

The same pair of events can be joined as two assertions or linked so one depends on the other. Compare a tomorrow phrase with an after-work construction; the phrase situates a plan without adding a predicate.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

If a learner labels every pair of propositions as coordination, ask which part sets the condition, reason or time for the other. If they reverse the dependency, reorder the ideas conceptually before retrying with a reviewed frame.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for reviewed alternatives, contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

**Targeted retry.** If the learner produces an additive pair when asked for a reason, ask which event explains the other. Retry by identifying the main claim and attaching the reason through a reviewed causal frame.

## 9. Natural usage

Ask the learner to decide what they will do if it rains, then explain a previous decision and arrange when they will leave. Compare each answer with two independent coordinated messages.

Draft exchange for review. The answer supplies a condition on tomorrow’s plan. Review tense and interpretation in the two-turn setting.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Required: understand both propositions.
- Helpful: recognise a main clause.
- Co-teach: linking frames and relation interpretation.
- Extension: detailed conditionals, non-finite forms and reported clauses.

Understanding of each proposition and the intended dependency. Co-teach a reviewed linking frame. Treat advanced non-finite and reporting structures as extensions. Requirements vary by outcome and depth. Use contextual audio and glosses for oral tasks so learners can demonstrate the target without script literacy.

## 12. Activity types

- Match plans with causes, conditions or times.
- Bracket dependency and compare with coordination.
- Express a new weather-dependent plan or temporal arrangement.

### Draft activity prototype

- **Prompt.** Give a conditional plan, and then explain a past decision caused by rain.
- **Expected response.** पानी पर्‍यो भने म घरमै बस्छु। / Paani paryo bhane ma gharmai baschhu. / If it rains, I will stay at home. पानी परेकाले म घरमै बसेँ। / Paani parekaale ma gharmai basẽ. / I stayed at home because it rained.
- **Feedback.** Mark the main claim and dependent clause in each. Use the after-work example in §6 for a temporal retry.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Give a new scenario and have the learner choose and produce a dependency frame without a model. Check that the intended consequence, reason or timing is preserved and that a coordinate paraphrase alone has not supplied the target evidence.

**Coverage check.** Assess identification of main and dependent clauses and construction in at least two reviewed frames, with an unseen interpretation example for the third. Score dependency independently of precise morphology.
