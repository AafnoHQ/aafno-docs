## 3. Meaning and usage

A self-related form may link back to a participant, foreground who acts or imply acting without help. These readings depend on construction and context.

## 4. Formation rules

For the mirror example map the looker and the person seen. They are the same participant. For the first reply, compare an offer to take over with an offer to help; the actor is highlighted in the first, while the second can convey doing the task without help.

**Worked models.** म आफैँ गर्छु। / Ma aafaĩ garchhu. / I'll do it myself. Compare रामले आफूलाई ऐनामा देख्यो। / Raamle aafulaai ainaamaa dekhyo. / Ram saw himself in the mirror. Use the situations in section 6 to establish the intended readings.

## 5. Variations and agreement

आफू / Aafu / self and आफैँ / Aafaĩ / oneself or personally require construction-specific analysis. Do not assume that the nearest noun or grammatical subject always controls interpretation. आफ्नो / Aafno / one’s own belongs to reflexive possession.

### Antecedent check

In the report in row 5, write out the reporter and addressee as separate candidates. The draft English version follows the reporter reading. Before scoring an alternative, add a preceding turn that makes one candidate salient, then ask speakers whether the other remains possible. If the evidence stays inconclusive, accept a clarification request as discourse evidence and withhold an antecedent verdict. A separate recognition probe can ask which self-related form is present in rows 1 and 5 without asking who it refers to.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

**Likely misunderstanding.** Treating every self-form as the object of an action obscures the actor-focused or independent-action reading in the task reply.

**Retry.** Trace the reflection to the looker and connect it to the self-form. For the contrast target, ask whether the person acts on themself or volunteers to perform the task. Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

**Draft usage scenario.** A colleague offers to complete a task and hears row 1. Reuse the reply after an offer of help and compare the interpretation. In a retelling about the mirror, use row 2 to keep reflexive object evidence separate. Check the turns with speakers in the intended context before using them as learner-facing models; no frequency or distribution is inferred.

## 10. Prerequisites

- **Required.** an understood actor and predicate.
- **Helpful.** names or pronouns for describing participants.
- **Co-taught.** antecedent tracking and contextual cueing.
- **Extension.** embedded reporting, possession and case.

For oral activities, supply enough context through audio or the scene so Devanagari literacy is not an assessment prerequisite.

## 12. Activity types

Row references below refer to the contextual examples in section 6. Use all three language representations by default; adjust visibility only for the evidence target.

### Initial target

- **Learner prompt.** Who is seen in the mirror example?
- **Expected response.** Ram is both looker and person seen.
- **Assessment target.** LO2. Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** Trace the reflection to the looker and connect it to the self-form.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

### Contrast target

- **Learner prompt.** How can the first reply be understood after each of the two offers?
- **Expected response.** It can highlight who acts or suggest acting without help; the task itself is not a reflexive object.
- **Assessment target.** LO3. Record the response against this target separately from the other prototype.
- **Feedback and retry.** Ask whether the person acts on themself or volunteers to perform the task.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Review the scenario, intended reading and accepted responses before use.

### Recognise a self-related form

- **Learner prompt.** In the reply to an offer in row 1, select the self-related form without deciding why the speaker used it.
- **Expected response.** आफैँ / Aafaĩ / personally or oneself.
- **Assessment target.** LO1, form recognition. Record antecedent and interpretation evidence only in the other activities.
- **Feedback and retry.** Replay the reply and ask which expression highlights the speaker's own participation. On retry, select that expression without explaining its reading.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm the audio cue, Romanisation and acceptable segmentation.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Use new self-directed, actor-emphasis, possession and reciprocal examples, and a multi-participant scene. Require a supported antecedent or justified uncertainty. Record what the answer demonstrates against the local outcomes and keep stem, ending, case, agreement and related semantic evidence separate. Guided performance can inform feedback; mastery requires an independent task and flexible evidence where relevant.
