## 3. Meaning and usage

Position describes where an expression occurs. Attachment identifies what it modifies, and scope concerns how much of the meaning it affects. Moving a word can preserve the basic proposition, change emphasis, or change a reading in context.

## 4. Formation rules

Compare the first two study reports with time near the subject or at the start. Hold the event fixed and ask what ‘today’ situates. Then compare two bracketed targets for the quantity/degree word and identify why the noun amount differs from an activity-level reading.

**Worked models.** म आज धेरै पढ्छु। / Ma aaja dherai padhchhu. / I will read or study a lot today. (Time within the clause; extent reading needs context.) Compare आज म धेरै पढ्छु। / Aaja ma dherai padhchhu. / Today I will read or study a lot. (Time-first candidate; contextual emphasis under review.) The examples are instructional candidates.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Context, prosody, construction and negation can affect interpretation. Do not treat a preferred location as a rule or claim all orders are interchangeable. Treat proposed scope effects as hypotheses for review.

### Scope under negation

Use a reviewed negative study report in two oral contexts: one where the speaker denies studying much and one where the speaker denies studying on the named day. Ask which part of the interpretation falls under the denial and what prosody or lexical change speakers would require. These English descriptions define test scenarios, not claims that the same Nepali wording licenses both. Collect the actual contrast before scoring an answer.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

A learner can sound plausible while using a cue unrelated to the target. Test what they inferred with the two independent prompts in section 12.

**Corrective cue for the first prompt.** Mark the subject and time expression before comparing positions.

**Corrective cue for the second prompt.** Bracket the host of the modifier and retry by spelling out the meaning instead of using position alone.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) if context permits more than one answer or the variant has not been reviewed.

## 12. Activity types

- **Expected response.** The books are quantified in row 3. Goodness is intensified in row 4.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Credit location, attachment and interpretation separately. A location label does not show semantic scope.

### Additional evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** The time-first report follows a question about today’s plans. Does moving the time phrase establish a different study day or a different number of books?
- **Expected response.** No such change is established in this draft context. Both examples situate study today. The time-first order may affect what is prominent.
- **Assessment target.** LO3 only. Credit location, attachment and interpretation separately. A location label does not show semantic scope.
- **Feedback and retry.** Separate which day is asserted from emphasis and quantity. Retry after a lead-in that asks about a different detail.
