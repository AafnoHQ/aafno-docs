## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise a stance expression.
- LO2: Interpret relative confidence in context.
- LO3: Distinguish confidence from source, inference and modality.
- LO4: Choose a stance for a scenario.
- LO5: Produce a reviewed confidence or uncertainty expression.

## 3. Meaning and usage

A stance adverb can qualify how confident a speaker sounds about a proposition. It need not tell the listener how the speaker knows or what conditions make the event possible. No fixed percentage follows from the examples.

## 4. Formation rules

Hold a coming event constant and compare a confident and a tentative report. Then keep the wording fixed while changing what the speaker heard or inferred; confidence alone does not resolve the evidence source.

**Worked models.** ऊ पक्कै आउँछ। / U pakkai aauchha. / He or she will definitely come. (A confident speaker in this scenario; source not stated.) Compare ऊ सायद आउँछ। / U saayad aauchha. / He or she might come. (Tentative prediction; source not stated.) The examples are instructional candidates.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Review register, intonation, scope and hedging. Expressions of ability, permission or possibility under G11 and evidential or inference patterns under G27 are related but separate.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

A learner can sound plausible while using a cue unrelated to the target. Test what they inferred with the two independent prompts in section 12.

**Corrective cue for the first prompt.** Replay both with matched delivery and ask which cue expresses stance.

**Corrective cue for the second prompt.** Ask first about confidence, then find any actual evidence-source cue before retrying.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) if context permits more than one answer or the variant has not been reviewed.

## 12. Activity types

References to rows are to section 6. Provide Devanagari, Romanisation and English by default. For unaided production, hide examples and target expressions until after the response. A scaffolded response is recorded as guided practice.

### First evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** Which speaker sounds more confident in the first two reports?
- **Expected response.** The speaker in row 1, in the intended context.
- **Assessment target.** LO2 only. Credit stance separately from the source of evidence and modality.
- **Feedback and retry.** Replay both with matched delivery and ask which cue expresses stance.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.

### Second evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** Can you tell from either report whether the speaker heard the news or inferred it? Identify the clue that would be needed.
- **Expected response.** The expressions mark confidence. Neither specifies the source on its own. An explicit reporting or inference cue, or contextual evidence, would be needed.
- **Assessment target.** LO3 only. Credit stance separately from the source of evidence and modality.
- **Feedback and retry.** Ask first about confidence, then find any actual evidence-source cue before retrying.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.

### Additional evidence target

- **Learner prompt.** Give a tentative Nepali prediction that the person will come, with no stance form or model displayed.
- **Expected response.** ऊ सायद आउँछ। / U saayad aauchha. / He or she might come.
- **Assessment target.** LO5 only. Credit stance separately from the source of evidence and modality.
- **Feedback and retry.** If the answer sounds categorical, recall that the speaker is unsure. Retry by expressing that stance without adding a claim about the source.
- **Validation status.** Draft. Confirm naturalness, context and acceptable responses before scored use.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Interpret stance in unseen paired reports, distinguish it from evidence or ability in another item and elicit a tentative report without displaying a model. Record what learners actually identify, interpret, select or produce against the applicable local outcome. Keep uncertainty visible and avoid inferring one outcome from evidence for another.
