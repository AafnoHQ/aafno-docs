## 2. Learning outcomes

- LO1: Recognise common foundational patterns in speech or writing.
- LO2: Interpret who does what in a subject–object–verb sentence.
- LO3: Construct a basic transitive sentence with the predicate in an appropriate position.
- LO4: Identify and construct a common objectless pattern.
- LO5: Interpret and construct a basic descriptive or state predicate.
- LO6: Transfer these patterns to new familiar-level vocabulary and settings.

## 3. Meaning and usage

Teach S–O–V as a common starting arrangement for transitive clauses. It helps a learner connect a participant, what they act on and the predicate. An objectless action or a state can express a complete idea with a different frame.

## 4. Formation rules

Start with the intended proposition and a familiar predicate. In the model about rice, म / Ma / I is the subject, भात / Bhaat / rice is the object, and खान्छु / Khaanchhu / eat is the finite verb. Build a transitive clause with an object where the frame calls for one. For a familiar intransitive clause, combine the subject and the relevant predicate. A state may use a descriptive element with a form of ‘be’.

## 5. Variations and agreement

Use agreement cues to identify the intended participant and keep the selected verb frame intact. A recoverable subject may remain implicit. Case, register and agreement are relevant to whether the completed sentence works. Review subject and predicate placement and natural alternatives by context.

## 8. Anticipated mistakes and corrective feedback

English-influenced order can place the verb before its object in a neutral routine. Ask the learner to locate the food or thing acted on and rebuild the illustrated frame. If a learner adds an unnecessary object to a motion verb, ask where they went and distinguish a destination from what was acted upon.

Use the [variant-aware assessment policy](../proficiency-framework.md#variant-aware-assessment) for reviewed alternatives, contextual mismatches and unreviewed forms.

## 9. Natural usage

Assign a familiar narrative prompt about a meal, bedtime or mood. Elicit two different frames in one exchange and let the listener identify what happened or what state was described.

Draft exchange for review. Compare the reply with a motion or state answer. The predicate frame determines the roles the learner needs to express.

| Turn | Devanagari | Romanisation | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Question | के खान्छौ? | Ke khaanchhau? | What do you eat? |
| Reply | म भात खान्छु। | Ma bhaat khaanchhu. | I eat rice. |

## 10. Prerequisites

Familiar predicate meanings; basic subject, object and verb roles can be co-taught. Extend to unfamiliar frames only after reviewing their argument structures. Requirements vary by outcome and depth. Use contextual audio and glosses for oral tasks so learners can demonstrate the target without script literacy.

## 12. Activity types

- Sort transitive, intransitive and descriptive frames.
- Arrange a meal or reading sentence and check whether a direct object is needed.
- Elicit separate spoken and written examples using unfamiliar reviewed verbs.

## 13. Mastery evidence

Use a novel meal or reading prompt and a separate motion or mood prompt. Assess meaning and construction of each frame independently. A label such as SOV or an accurate ending in isolation does not establish construction evidence.
